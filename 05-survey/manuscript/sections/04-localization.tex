\section{Localization and Mapping}
\label{sec:localization}

Localization and mapping responses to degradation differ in the information they retain and in their assumptions about geometry, contact, and correlations between estimates (Table~\ref{S-tab:localization} in the supplementary material).

\subsection{Localizability and Selective Measurement Updates}

Under geometric degeneracy, LODESTAR keeps the information that remains in the weak directions by selecting historical Schmidt--Kalman anchors from the condition of the LiDAR measurement Jacobian and choosing points by their contribution along those directions~\cite{P_E01}. With a common FAST-LIO2 backbone, absolute pose error (APE), an ATE from the EVO tool with no stated alignment or statistic, on the Long Corridor recording of the SubT-MRS dataset~\cite{P_K01} falls from 3.113 to 1.386\,m, while average desktop computation rises from 21.04 to 28.69\,ms. The improvement is not uniform, since the APE on the Short exp recording is 0.429\,m against 0.412\,m for the baseline. Other methods characterize degeneracy explicitly, through screw-theoretic classification of translational, helical, and spherical degeneracy~\cite{P_E05} or a DBSCAN-based detector that adapts to condition-number history and compensates with IMU motion after a startup phase~\cite{P_S31}. Others condition it, through normal-histogram feature selection along weak tunnel directions in DR-ICP~\cite{P_S24} or through DCReg, which preconditions the weak subspaces without changing the least-squares objective~\cite{P_S37}. Such conditioning exploits weak but nonzero information without restoring an absent constraint or rescuing an initialization outside the convergence basin.

\subsection{Complementary Sensing}

Complementary sensors differ mainly in when the second modality enters the estimate. Mixed degradation remains difficult, since no sensor-fusion entry met the success criteria in the SubT-MRS challenge, whose sequences come from aerial, legged, and wheeled robots~\cite{P_K01}. Its LiDAR entries averaged 1.61\,m ATE on mixed-degradation sequences against 0.836\,m on geometric ones. Switch-SLAM adds visual constraints only when normalized Hessian eigenvalues indicate LiDAR degeneracy and falls back to inertial propagation when both modalities fail~\cite{P_F04}. Degradation-resilient LiDAR--radar--inertial odometry fuses continuously, keeping LiDAR constraints along observable directions and using radar velocity as independent motion information in obscurant tests on a flying robot~\cite{P_E09}. An equivariant LiDAR--radar--inertial filter estimates inertial biases and rejects radar outliers, with observability resting on an exact map~\cite{P_E06}. Radar-centric estimators must also cope with sparse, noisy returns, as a continuous-trajectory radar--inertial estimator propagates pose uncertainty to each point timestamp and down-weights uninformative points~\cite{P_R01}, whereas Super4DR learns radar odometry by self-supervision and completes the radar map with Gaussian primitives partly guided by image depth~\cite{P_R12}. Teammates provide another source, as RaLI-Multi corrects LiDAR--inertial drift along the detected degenerate direction with inter-vehicle UWB ranges, provided that at least one horizontal direction remains well constrained~\cite{P_R02}. When no modality remains informative, MM-LINS hibernates the unreliable map, reinitializes, and registers the maps once they overlap again~\cite{P_E08}.

\subsection{Motion Priors, Contact, and Adaptation}

Ground geometry enters estimation as a reweighting of ground and non-ground corrections in AESF-LIO, which adds no independent observation~\cite{P_N01}. It enters as a planar prior in SE2-LIO, which parameterizes pose on $SE(2)$ as in \eqref{eq:unicycle}~\cite{P_N02}, or as an explicit ground-normal factor in a sparse-channel LiDAR system whose ground detector misses gradual slope changes~\cite{P_R03}. All three designs rely on near-planar ground.

Wheel and inertial measurements bridge failures of external sensing while contact and excitation remain adequate. Wheel-aided VIO propagates inertial--wheel motion during visual failure~\cite{P_M24}, while PL-VIWO adds motion-consistency rejection and point-assisted line triangulation with its calibration held fixed~\cite{P_E10}. Ground models extend this propagation beyond flat floors. A quadratic motion-manifold estimator integrates wheel measurements on a local ground model that extremely uneven farmland violates~\cite{P_M43}, whereas WING learns IMU-bias and wheel-error corrections for skidding and vertical bouncing on a continuous B-spline ground manifold, yielding interoceptive-only odometry~\cite{P_R04}.

Contact uncertainty can also be estimated explicitly, as when a tire-model observer fuses wheel-level velocities by covariance intersection and excludes inconsistent wheels~\cite{P_M39}. Time-of-flight (ToF)--wheel odometry inflates process covariance only along the remainder that the differential-drive model \eqref{eq:differential} cannot explain~\cite{P_E02}. DURAL switches between LiDAR--inertial--UWB estimation inside a surveyed anchor region and wheel-aided estimation outside it~\cite{P_E03}. Learned kinematic factors~\cite{P_E04} and the hierarchical modality reweighting of Super Odometry~\cite{P_E07} train motion priors while perception is reliable and freeze them when supervision degrades.

Lug-generated wheel traces reveal slip on suitable soil~\cite{P_N03}, whereas commanded collisions with known-map geometry constrain pose in a filter, tested on hardware with manually commanded contacts~\cite{P_N19}. GNSS-aided IMU--odometer calibration estimates wheel scale and extrinsics but leaves vertical translation unobservable under planar motion~\cite{P_S32}. Such calibration parameters should not absorb transient contact failure. IL-LIO learns point-to-voxel residuals and uncertainty for nonplanar geometry~\cite{P_S30}, although learned residual variance is not an independently calibrated integrity bound.

\subsection{Dynamic Geometry and Persistent Maps}

Dynamic-scene estimators either exclude moving observations or estimate them. Exclusion keeps the static map clean but can remove much of the geometry in crowds, as in semantic rejection of person features in hospital RGB-D SLAM~\cite{P_M44} and in GPU-free asynchronous mask propagation in NGD-SLAM, whose reported tracking time excludes concurrent detection~\cite{P_E19}. DynoSAM instead estimates rigid-object motion in world-centric factor graphs through full-batch optimization without loop closure~\cite{P_E15}. PG-SLAM and JPG-SLAM retain dynamic appearance through Gaussian representations, although neither runs in real time with loop closure~\cite{P_E17,P_E20}. Improved rendering does not by itself establish a better obstacle map.

Across sessions, persistence enters as a survival prior on semantic clusters in ProbPer-LiLo~\cite{P_E16} and as local and cross-session ephemerality weights for registration in ELite~\cite{P_E18}. A lifelong LiDAR mapper instead stores positive and negative map differences, from which earlier versions can be reconstructed~\cite{P_E11}. Chamelion learns change detection from synthesized structural changes and separates disappearance from occlusion before updating construction-site and office maps, performing best when registration error and voxel size resemble its training data~\cite{P_R07}. For automotive radar, multi-session loop verification and a temporal occupancy grid retain persistent points for later localization under a planar-trajectory assumption~\cite{P_R09}. NVMS-SLAM keeps normal-bearing surfaces apart to avoid merging opposite faces of thin walls and refines alignment offline~\cite{P_S01}, while NeuSE updates object maps after layout changes within the categories on which it was trained~\cite{P_S34}.

\subsection{Prior-Map and Cross-Modal Localization}

Localization against a compact or structural prior reduces the online representation but becomes sensitive to changes in the mapped environment. ERPoT matches sparse scans to polygon maps after ground removal~\cite{P_M13}, Area Graph localization matches filtered scans to walls and doors after WiFi and barometric initialization~\cite{P_S03}, and building information model (BIM) localization estimates as-designed discrepancies before weighting structural constraints~\cite{P_S36}. Orchard trunk maps suppress seasonal foliage at the price of making global recovery depend on adequate trunk support~\cite{P_S22}. An uncertainty-aware outdoor localizer weights normal distributions transform (NDT) matching by estimated covariance and gates GNSS, a rule that can reject accurate fixes after extended LiDAR loss~\cite{P_M16}.

Relocalization obtains pose hypotheses from appearance, through cross-attention image retrieval on pushed-robot recordings~\cite{P_M21} or distilled LiDAR--camera place recognition~\cite{P_E56}. Structural hypotheses come from object-level scene graphs that tolerate staged object moves~\cite{P_M34}, rendered point-cloud, mesh, or neural-radiance maps~\cite{P_S26}, and density maps for loop closure that can inherit front-end degeneracy~\cite{P_S35}. Retrieval provides a candidate rather than a verified pose.

Cross-modal matching relaxes the requirement that map and query share a sensor. CMRNext casts camera-to-LiDAR matching as optical flow followed by perspective-n-point estimation and transfers across camera setups, although it is trained on driving data, does not yet run in real time, and receives an initial pose for every frame~\cite{P_R06}. OMCL scores Monte Carlo hypotheses with open-vocabulary features ray-cast from a three-dimensional map and can initialize from a language description, provided that odometry is map-scaled and the map features still match the scene, since the map is not updated online~\cite{P_R10}. LiDAR Teach, Radar Repeat localizes forward-looking 4D radar against LiDAR keyframes from a teaching pass, with registration errors driving label-free fine-tuning~\cite{P_R11}. Its route repetition on a four-wheel differential-drive platform covers smoke, traffic, and structural change over months, with obstacle avoidance left to other sensors.

\subsection{Cooperative Estimates, Uncertain Maps, and Evaluation Resources}

Cooperative estimators add constraints while introducing correlations. CoVOR-SLAM corrects monocular scale with inter-agent ranges in a similarity-pose graph~\cite{P_F15}, while a Lie-group estimator tracks the cross-covariances among wheel, inertial, and marker estimates over near-straight indoor motion~\cite{P_E53}. For uncertainty in the map itself, semantic neural processes predict elevation with an uncertainty that still requires calibration~\cite{P_S46}, whereas E2-BKI propagates evidential semantic uncertainty through geometry-aligned kernels~\cite{P_R08}. A multi-fidelity Gaussian process (GP) explorer on an aquatic robot weights field samples by localization uncertainty~\cite{P_F10}.

Robot-mounted lighting in LUCID improves visual appearance without necessarily improving odometry~\cite{P_S20}. AirSLAM combines learned point and line features with map reuse and reaches 40\,Hz on an embedded GPU under illumination change, although it reduces to a point-feature system where structural lines are absent~\cite{P_R05}.
