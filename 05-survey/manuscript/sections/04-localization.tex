\section{Localization and Mapping}
\label{sec:localization}

Localization and mapping methods respond to degradation through selective measurement updates, complementary sensing, motion priors, relocalization, and map revision. These responses differ in the information they retain and in their assumptions about geometry, contact, and correlations between estimates. Table~\ref{S-tab:localization} in the supplementary material lists representative implementations with their inputs, evaluation settings, and computation placement.

\subsection{Localizability and Selective Measurement Updates}

Geometric degeneracy occurs when measurements constrain some pose directions much less than others. One response keeps the information that remains in the weak directions, as LODESTAR does by selecting historical Schmidt--Kalman anchors from the condition of the LiDAR measurement Jacobian and choosing points by their contribution along those directions~\cite{P_E01}. With a common FAST-LIO2 backbone, absolute pose error (APE) on the SubT-MRS Long Corridor recording falls from 3.113 to 1.386\,m, at the cost of an increase in average desktop computation from 21.04 to 28.69\,ms. However, the improvement is not uniform, since the APE on the Short exp recording is 0.429\,m against 0.412\,m for the baseline. Other detectors characterize degeneracy before acting on it. A screw-theoretic analysis classifies translational, helical, and spherical degeneracy from point, line, range, and bearing constraints~\cite{P_E05}. DR-ICP uses normal histograms to select and weight features along weak tunnel directions~\cite{P_S24}, whereas a DBSCAN-based detector adapts its decision to accumulated condition-number history and compensates with IMU motion after a startup phase~\cite{P_S31}. DCReg decouples registration and preconditions the weak subspaces without changing the least-squares objective~\cite{P_S37}. Such conditioning exploits weak but nonzero information, although it neither restores an absent constraint nor rescues an initialization outside the convergence basin. Comparisons of selection, compensation, and preconditioning therefore need controls matched to the information that each method exploits.

\subsection{Complementary Sensing}

Complementary sensors supply information that a degraded scan lacks, and they differ mainly in when the second modality enters the estimate. Switch-SLAM adds visual constraints only when normalized Hessian eigenvalues indicate LiDAR degeneracy and falls back to inertial propagation when both modalities fail~\cite{P_F04}. Degradation-resilient LiDAR--radar--inertial odometry fuses continuously, keeping LiDAR constraints along observable directions and using radar velocity as independent motion information in obscurant tests on a flying robot~\cite{P_E09}. An equivariant LiDAR--radar--inertial filter estimates inertial biases and rejects radar outliers, with observability resting on an exact map~\cite{P_E06}. Radar-centric estimators must also cope with sparse, noisy returns. A continuous-trajectory radar--inertial estimator propagates pose uncertainty to the timestamp of each point and down-weights uninformative points during registration and mapping~\cite{P_R01}, whereas Super4DR learns radar odometry through self-supervision and completes the radar map with Gaussian primitives guided partly by image depth~\cite{P_R12}. Teammates provide another source of information. RaLI-Multi corrects LiDAR--inertial drift along the detected degenerate direction with inter-vehicle UWB ranges, provided that at least one horizontal direction remains well constrained, and a stopped anchor vehicle optimizes and broadcasts the shared pose graph~\cite{P_R02}. When no modality remains informative, MM-LINS hibernates the unreliable map, reinitializes, and registers the maps once they overlap again~\cite{P_E08}, trading continuous use of one map for recovery through later overlap. Selective updates need surviving geometry, fusion needs measurements that remain informative, and reinitialization needs later overlap, none of which a degeneracy alarm alone establishes.

\subsection{Motion Priors, Contact, and Adaptation}

Ground geometry can enter estimation as a reweighting, a motion prior, or an explicit factor. AESF-LIO reweights by fusing separate ground and non-ground corrections, which adds no independent observation~\cite{P_N01}. SE2-LIO imposes a planar prior by parameterizing pose on $SE(2)$ and modeling out-of-plane perturbations as noise~\cite{P_N02}. A sparse-channel LiDAR system adds an explicit ground-normal factor and intensity features to suppress vertical drift, although its ground detector misses gradual slope changes~\cite{P_R03}. All three designs rely on near-planar ground, which significant slope variation can invalidate.

Wheel and inertial measurements bridge failures of external sensing when contact and excitation remain adequate. Wheel-aided VIO propagates inertial--wheel motion during visual failure~\cite{P_M24}, while PL-VIWO adds motion-consistency rejection and point-assisted line triangulation to visual--inertial--wheel odometry, with its calibration held fixed~\cite{P_E10}. Ground models extend this propagation beyond flat floors, as in a quadratic motion-manifold estimator that integrates wheel measurements on a local ground model, which extremely uneven farmland violates~\cite{P_M43}. WING learns corrections for IMU bias and for wheel errors caused by skidding and vertical bouncing on a continuous B-spline ground manifold, yielding interoceptive-only odometry whose value as a fallback depends on how closely the ground follows the manifold~\cite{P_R04}.

Contact uncertainty can also be estimated explicitly, as when a tire-model observer fuses wheel-level velocity estimates by covariance intersection and excludes inconsistent wheels~\cite{P_M39}. Time-of-flight (ToF)--wheel odometry inflates process covariance only along the remainder that differential-drive motion cannot explain~\cite{P_E02}. DURAL switches between LiDAR--inertial--UWB estimation inside a surveyed anchor region and wheel-aided estimation outside it~\cite{P_E03}. Learned kinematic factors~\cite{P_E04} and the hierarchical modality reweighting of Super Odometry~\cite{P_E07} train motion priors while perception is reliable and freeze them when supervision degrades, which limits the incorporation of unreliable measurements.

Wheel traces, deliberate contact, and calibration provide additional observations. Lug-generated wheel traces reveal slip on suitable soil~\cite{P_N03}, whereas commanded collisions with known-map geometry constrain pose in a filter, with hardware tests that rely on manually commanded contacts~\cite{P_N19}. GNSS-aided IMU--odometer calibration estimates wheel scale and extrinsics but leaves vertical translation unobservable under planar motion~\cite{P_S32}. Such calibration parameters should not absorb transient contact failure. IL-LIO learns point-to-voxel residuals and uncertainty for nonplanar geometry~\cite{P_S30}, although learned residual variance is not an independently calibrated integrity bound.

\subsection{Dynamic Geometry and Persistent Maps}

Dynamic-scene estimators choose between excluding moving observations and estimating them. Exclusion keeps the static map clean but can remove much of the geometry in crowds. Examples include semantic rejection of person features in hospital RGB-D SLAM~\cite{P_M44} and GPU-free asynchronous mask propagation in NGD-SLAM, whose reported tracking time excludes concurrent detection~\cite{P_E19}. DynoSAM instead estimates rigid-object motion in world-centric factor graphs through full-batch optimization without loop closure~\cite{P_E15}. PG-SLAM and JPG-SLAM retain dynamic appearance through Gaussian representations, although neither runs in real time with loop closure~\cite{P_E17,P_E20}. Improved rendering does not by itself establish a better obstacle map, since the appropriate representation depends on whether the downstream task needs static geometry or object motion. Dense static maps carry their own cost, as dense multi-scan adjustment fits grid-cell distributions jointly over several scans and runs slower than recording time~\cite{P_N20}.

Across sessions, map maintenance additionally requires an estimate of how long structure persists. Persistence enters as a survival prior on semantic clusters in ProbPer-LiLo~\cite{P_E16} and as local and cross-session ephemerality weights for registration in ELite~\cite{P_E18}. A lifelong LiDAR mapper instead stores positive and negative map differences, from which earlier versions can be reconstructed~\cite{P_E11}. Chamelion learns change detection from synthesized structural changes and separates disappearance from occlusion before updating construction-site and office maps, performing best when registration error and voxel size resemble its training data~\cite{P_R07}. For automotive radar, multi-session loop verification and a temporal occupancy grid retain persistent points for later localization under a planar-trajectory assumption~\cite{P_R09}. NVMS-SLAM keeps normal-bearing surfaces apart to avoid merging opposite faces of thin walls and refines alignment offline~\cite{P_S01}, while NeuSE updates object maps after layout changes within the categories on which it was trained~\cite{P_S34}. Most of these evaluations use recorded data, handheld acquisition, or injected changes, which leaves open how a moving robot responds to a revised map.

\subsection{Prior-Map and Cross-Modal Localization}

Localization against a compact or structural prior reduces the online representation but becomes sensitive to changes in the mapped environment. ERPoT matches sparse scans to polygon maps after ground removal~\cite{P_M13}, Area Graph localization matches filtered scans to walls and doors after WiFi and barometric initialization~\cite{P_S03}, and building information model (BIM) localization estimates as-designed discrepancies before weighting structural constraints~\cite{P_S36}. Orchard trunk maps suppress seasonal foliage at the price of making global recovery depend on adequate trunk support~\cite{P_S22}. An uncertainty-aware outdoor localizer weights normal distributions transform (NDT) matching by estimated covariance and gates GNSS, a rule that can reject accurate fixes after extended LiDAR loss~\cite{P_M16}.

Relocalization obtains new pose hypotheses from appearance or structure. Appearance cues include cross-attention image retrieval on pushed-robot recordings~\cite{P_M21} and distilled LiDAR--camera place recognition~\cite{P_E56}. Structural cues include object-level scene graphs that tolerate staged object moves~\cite{P_M34}, rendered point-cloud, mesh, or neural-radiance maps~\cite{P_S26}, and density maps for loop closure, which can inherit front-end degeneracy~\cite{P_S35}. Retrieval provides a candidate rather than a verified pose, which the recovery procedure must then validate.

Cross-modal matching relaxes the requirement that map and query share a sensor. CMRNext casts camera-to-LiDAR matching as optical flow followed by perspective-n-point estimation and transfers across camera setups, although it is trained on driving data, does not yet run in real time, and receives an initial pose for every frame~\cite{P_R06}. OMCL scores Monte Carlo hypotheses with open-vocabulary features ray-cast from a three-dimensional map and can initialize from a language description, provided that odometry is map-scaled and the map features still match the scene, since the map is not updated online~\cite{P_R10}. LiDAR Teach, Radar Repeat applies such cross-modal registration within a closed-loop route-repetition system~\cite{P_R11}. Forward-looking 4D radar is localized against LiDAR keyframes from a teaching pass using a Doppler motion prior and an alignment of radar power with LiDAR intensity, with registration errors driving label-free fine-tuning. Route repetition on a four-wheel differential-drive platform covers smoke, moving traffic, and structural change over months, with obstacle avoidance left to other sensors.

\subsection{Cooperative Estimates, Uncertain Maps, and Evaluation Resources}

Cooperative estimators add constraints while introducing correlations. CoVOR-SLAM corrects monocular scale with inter-agent ranges in a similarity-pose graph~\cite{P_F15}, while a Lie-group estimator tracks the cross-covariances among wheel, inertial, and marker estimates over near-straight indoor motion~\cite{P_E53}. A fault-tolerant prior-map estimator diagnoses observation, landmark, and odometry faults and lets a repaired landmark benefit a later collaborator~\cite{P_F03}. Uncertainty in the map itself is represented in other ways. Semantic neural processes predict elevation with an uncertainty that still requires calibration~\cite{P_S46}, whereas E2-BKI propagates evidential semantic uncertainty through geometry-aligned kernels~\cite{P_R08}. Pose uncertainty can also weight the mapped quantity, as in a multi-fidelity Gaussian process (GP) explorer that weights field samples on an aquatic robot by localization uncertainty~\cite{P_F10}. How a calibrated pose distribution should change wheel-footprint clearance or speed remains open, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:challenges}.

Benchmarks and active sensing qualify robustness claims. M2DGR and M2UD combine sensing and motion difficulties on ground robots~\cite{P_M41,P_F11}, while HK-MEMS and GEODE vary degeneracy, sensor carriers, and reference construction~\cite{P_S21,P_S33}. Active sensing adds a caution, since robot-mounted lighting in LUCID improves visual appearance without necessarily improving odometry~\cite{P_S20}. AirSLAM combines learned point and line features with map reuse and reaches 40\,Hz on an embedded GPU under illumination change, although it reduces to a point-feature system where structural lines are absent~\cite{P_R05}.

\subsection{Synthesis}

The main distinction among these methods is which information survives degradation and how the estimator uses it. When degradation leaves weak but nonzero constraints, selective updates and preconditioning are the least invasive response, since they keep the sensor set and change only how its information is weighted. When a modality loses its information entirely, only an independent source such as radar velocity, ranging, or wheel and contact sensing restores the missing directions. Learned and geometric motion priors bridge such gaps only while their supervision remains credible and the ground follows their model. Relocalization and map revision address accumulated inconsistency rather than momentary weakness, with further requirements for overlap, persistence, cross-modal registration, and correlation handling. Their diagnostics also differ in meaning, since geometric conditioning, motion consistency, and place similarity cannot be treated as a common probability of localization failure. The evidence for these responses stops at the estimate, since only \LocTaskCount{} of the \LocTechCount{} studies reviewed in this section report a task outcome (Table~\ref{tab:evaluation}). The consequences of each response for planning and control therefore remain unmeasured.
