\section{Localization and Mapping}
\label{sec:localization}

Localization and mapping methods respond to degradation through selective measurement updates, complementary sensing, motion priors, relocalization, and map revision. These responses differ in the information they retain and in their assumptions about geometry, contact, and correlations between estimates. \subsection{Localizability and selective measurement updates}

Geometric degeneracy occurs when measurements constrain some pose directions much less than others. LODESTAR retains information in the weak directions by selecting historical Schmidt--Kalman anchors from the condition of the LiDAR measurement Jacobian and by choosing points according to their contribution along those directions~\cite{P_E01}. With a common FAST-LIO2 backbone, absolute pose error (APE) on the SubT-MRS Long Corridor recording falls from 3.113 to 1.386\,m, while average desktop computation rises from 21.04 to 28.69\,ms. The ordering is not uniform, since on Short exp the APE is 0.429\,m versus 0.412\,m for the baseline, and the method still requires LiDAR returns. Other detectors characterize degeneracy before acting on it. A screw-theoretic analysis classifies translational, helical, and spherical degeneracy from point, line, range, and bearing constraints~\cite{P_E05}, normal histograms guide feature selection and weighting along weak tunnel directions in DR-ICP~\cite{P_S24}, and a DBSCAN-based detector adapts its decisions from accumulated condition-number history and compensates with IMU motion after a startup phase~\cite{P_S31}. Decoupled registration preconditions weak subspaces while preserving the least-squares objective~\cite{P_S37}. Better conditioning exploits weak but nonzero information without restoring an absent constraint or rescuing an initialization outside the convergence basin, which means that selection, compensation, and preconditioning require different controls when their benefits are compared.

\subsection{Complementary sensing}

Complementary sensors supply information that a degraded scan lacks. Switch-SLAM introduces visual constraints when normalized Hessian eigenvalues indicate LiDAR degeneracy and falls back to inertial propagation when both modalities fail~\cite{P_F04}. Degradation-resilient LiDAR--radar--inertial odometry keeps LiDAR constraints along observable directions while radar velocity supplies independent motion information, with flying-robot tests in obscurants~\cite{P_E09}, and an equivariant LiDAR--radar--inertial filter estimates inertial biases and rejects radar outliers under an exact-map observability assumption~\cite{P_E06}. Radar-centric estimators must also cope with sparse, noisy returns. A continuous-trajectory radar--inertial estimator propagates pose uncertainty to the timestamp of each point and down-weights uninformative points during registration and mapping~\cite{P_R01}, whereas Super4DR learns radar odometry through self-supervision and completes the radar map with Gaussian primitives guided partly by image depth~\cite{P_R12}. RaLI-Multi uses inter-vehicle UWB ranges to correct LiDAR--inertial drift along the detected degenerate direction, assuming at least one well-constrained horizontal direction, while a stopped anchor vehicle optimizes and broadcasts a shared pose graph~\cite{P_R02}. MM-LINS instead hibernates an unreliable map, reinitializes, and later registers overlapping maps~\cite{P_E08}, trading continuous use of one map for recovery through later overlap. Selective updates need surviving geometry, fusion needs measurements that remain informative, and reinitialization needs later overlap, and none of these requirements follows from a degeneracy alarm alone.

\subsection{Motion priors, contact, and adaptation}

Ground geometry can enter estimation as a reweighting, a motion prior, or an explicit factor. AESF-LIO adaptively fuses separate ground and non-ground corrections without adding an independent observation~\cite{P_N01}, SE2-LIO parameterizes pose on $SE(2)$ with out-of-plane perturbations modeled as noise~\cite{P_N02}, and a sparse-channel LiDAR system adds a ground-normal factor and intensity features to suppress vertical drift, although its detector misses gradual slope changes and its indoor references share LiDAR registration with the estimator~\cite{P_R03}. These designs address different error causes, and significant slope variation can invalidate the near-planar priors.

Wheel and inertial measurements bridge failures of external sensing when contact and excitation remain adequate. Wheel-aided VIO propagates inertial--wheel motion during visual failure~\cite{P_M24}, PL-VIWO adds motion-consistency rejection and point-assisted line triangulation evaluated on recorded vehicle data with fixed calibration~\cite{P_E10}, and a quadratic motion-manifold estimator integrates wheel measurements on a local ground model that extremely uneven farmland violates~\cite{P_M43}. WING learns corrections for IMU bias and wheel errors caused by skidding and vertical bouncing on a continuous B-spline ground manifold, which supports interoceptive-only odometry as a fallback whose benefit depends on how closely the ground follows the manifold~\cite{P_R04}. Contact uncertainty can also be estimated explicitly, as when a tire-model observer fuses wheel-level velocity estimates by covariance intersection and excludes inconsistent wheels~\cite{P_M39}, and time-of-flight (ToF)--wheel odometry inflates process covariance only along the remainder that differential-drive motion cannot explain~\cite{P_E02}. DURAL switches between LiDAR--inertial--UWB estimation inside a surveyed anchor region and wheel-aided estimation outside it~\cite{P_E03}. Learned kinematic factors~\cite{P_E04} and the hierarchical modality reweighting of Super Odometry~\cite{P_E07} train motion priors while perception is reliable and freeze them when supervision degrades, which limits the incorporation of unreliable measurements.

Contact and calibration provide additional observations. Lug-generated wheel traces reveal slip on suitable soil~\cite{P_N03}, and commanded collisions with known-map geometry constrain pose in a filter, although the hardware tests use manually commanded contacts~\cite{P_N19}. GNSS-aided IMU--odometer calibration estimates wheel scale and extrinsics but leaves vertical translation unobservable under planar motion~\cite{P_S32}, and a calibration parameter should not absorb transient contact failure. IL-LIO learns point-to-voxel residuals and uncertainty for nonplanar geometry~\cite{P_S30}, although learned residual variance is not an independently calibrated integrity bound.

\subsection{Dynamic geometry and persistent maps}

Dense multi-scan adjustment jointly fits grid-cell distributions over several scans and runs slower than recording time~\cite{P_N20}. Dynamic-scene estimators choose between excluding moving observations and estimating them. Exclusion keeps the static map clean but can remove much of the geometry in crowds, as in semantic rejection of person features in hospital RGB-D SLAM~\cite{P_M44} and in GPU-free asynchronous mask propagation in NGD-SLAM, whose reported tracking time excludes concurrent detection~\cite{P_E19}. DynoSAM instead estimates rigid-object motion in world-centric factor graphs, relying on full-batch optimization without loop closure~\cite{P_E15}, and PG-SLAM and JPG-SLAM retain dynamic appearance through Gaussian representations that are not real time or lack loop closure~\cite{P_E17,P_E20}. Improved rendering does not by itself establish a better obstacle map, and the choice depends on whether the downstream task needs static geometry or object motion.

Across sessions, map maintenance additionally requires an estimate of how long structure persists. ProbPer-LiLo estimates semantic cluster persistence with a survival prior~\cite{P_E16}, ELite weights registration by local and cross-session ephemerality~\cite{P_E18}, and a lifelong LiDAR mapper stores positive and negative map differences that allow earlier versions to be reconstructed~\cite{P_E11}. Chamelion learns change detection from synthesized structural changes and separates disappearance from occlusion before updating construction-site and office maps, performing best when registration error and voxel size resemble its training data~\cite{P_R07}. For automotive radar, multi-session loop verification and a temporal occupancy grid retain persistent points for later localization under a planar-trajectory assumption~\cite{P_R09}. NVMS-SLAM keeps normal-bearing surfaces apart to avoid merging opposite faces of thin walls and refines alignment offline~\cite{P_S01}, and NeuSE updates object maps after layout changes within trained categories~\cite{P_S34}. Most of these evaluations use recorded data, handheld acquisition, or injected changes, and map consistency alone does not establish how a moving robot responds to a revised map.

\subsection{Prior-map and cross-modal localization}

Localization against a compact or structural prior reduces the online representation but becomes sensitive to changes in the mapped environment. ERPoT matches sparse scans to polygon maps after ground removal~\cite{P_M13}, Area Graph localization matches filtered scans to walls and doors with WiFi and barometric initialization~\cite{P_S03}, BIM localization estimates as-designed discrepancies before weighting structural constraints~\cite{P_S36}, and orchard trunk maps suppress seasonal foliage while making global recovery depend on adequate trunk support~\cite{P_S22}. An uncertainty-aware outdoor localizer weights NDT matching by estimated covariance and gates GNSS, which can reject accurate fixes after extended LiDAR loss~\cite{P_M16}. Relocalization obtains new hypotheses from appearance or structure, including cross-attention image retrieval on pushed-robot recordings~\cite{P_M21}, object-level scene graphs that tolerate staged object moves~\cite{P_M34}, distilled LiDAR--camera place recognition~\cite{P_E56}, rendered point-cloud, mesh, or neural-radiance maps~\cite{P_S26}, and density-map loop closure whose maps can inherit front-end degeneracy~\cite{P_S35}. Retrieval provides a candidate rather than a verified pose, and recovery requires subsequent pose validation.

Cross-modal matching relaxes the requirement that map and query share a sensor. CMRNext casts camera-to-LiDAR matching as optical flow followed by perspective-n-point estimation and transfers across camera setups, although it is trained on driving data, is not yet real time, and receives an initial pose for every frame~\cite{P_R06}. OMCL scores Monte Carlo hypotheses with open-vocabulary features ray-cast from a three-dimensional map and can initialize from a language description, while requiring map-scaled odometry and a static map~\cite{P_R10}. LiDAR Teach, Radar Repeat applies cross-modal registration in closed loop~\cite{P_R11}. Forward-looking 4D radar is localized against LiDAR keyframes from a teaching pass using a Doppler motion prior and an alignment of radar power with LiDAR intensity, and registration errors drive label-free fine-tuning. Route repetition on a four-wheel differential-drive platform covers smoke, moving traffic, and structural change over months, while obstacle avoidance still relies on other sensors.

\subsection{Cooperative estimates and evaluation resources}

Cooperative estimators add constraints while introducing correlations. CoVOR-SLAM corrects monocular scale with inter-agent ranges in a similarity-pose graph evaluated on recorded data with one stationary rover~\cite{P_F15}, a Lie-group estimator propagates cross-covariances among wheel, inertial, and marker estimates over near-straight indoor motion~\cite{P_E53}, and a fault-tolerant prior-map estimator diagnoses observation, landmark, and odometry faults and lets a repaired landmark benefit a later collaborator~\cite{P_F03}. Benchmarks and active sensing qualify robustness claims. M2DGR and M2UD expose combined sensing and motion difficulties~\cite{P_M41,P_F11}, HK-MEMS and GEODE vary degeneracy, carriers, and reference construction~\cite{P_S21,P_S33}, and robot-mounted lighting in LUCID shows that improved visual appearance does not necessarily improve odometry~\cite{P_S20}. AirSLAM combines learned point and line features with map reuse and reaches 40\,Hz on an embedded GPU, although it reduces to a point-feature system without structural lines~\cite{P_R05}. Among uncertain terrain representations, semantic neural processes predict elevation with uncertainty that still requires calibration~\cite{P_S46}, E2-BKI propagates evidential semantic uncertainty through geometry-aligned kernels~\cite{P_R08}, and a multi-fidelity GP explorer weights field samples by localization uncertainty on an aquatic robot~\cite{P_F10}. How a calibrated pose distribution should change wheel-footprint clearance or speed remains open, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:challenges}.

\subsection{Synthesis}

The main distinction among these methods is which information survives degradation. An estimator can retain constraints in observable directions, add independent motion information from radar, range, wheel, or contact sensing, or bridge weak external sensing with learned or geometric priors whose adaptation is useful only while supervision remains credible. Relocalization and map revision correct accumulated inconsistency through new correspondences, with further requirements for overlap, persistence, cross-modal registration, and correlation handling. Their diagnostics differ in meaning, since geometric conditioning, motion consistency, and place similarity cannot be treated as a common probability of localization failure.
