\section{Semantic and Language-Guided Navigation}
\label{sec:semantic}

The output of a semantic navigation system, whether a room, an object instance, frontier scores, a path, a cost layer, or an action sequence, determines how a reasoning error propagates into planning and control (Table~\ref{S-tab:semantic} in the supplementary material).

\subsection{Semantic Memory and Scene Structure}

Persistent semantic maps support relational queries but must be updated when objects move. HOV-SG organizes a mapped building into floors, rooms, and objects for hierarchical retrieval~\cite{P_E48}, while DualMap searches stable anchors globally before checking objects locally~\cite{P_M02}. OpenIN updates the existence confidence of a carrier--object pair when an expected object is not observed~\cite{P_E44}. Such updates must distinguish removal from occlusion or poor depth. osmAG-LLM instead assumes from the outset that objects move, keeping a lightweight text-based area graph for context, asking a language model for likely locations, and confirming targets by online detection~\cite{P_R27}. In 30 building trials it succeeds end to end in 21 and reaches a correct position in 27, with detection errors causing most failures.

Goal-conditioned maps store less reusable structure, as VLFM projects image--language similarity into a frontier value map until a detector supplies the goal~\cite{P_E47}. VLN-Game builds an object-centric three-dimensional map and uses a game-theoretic vision-language model (VLM) to disambiguate attribute-rich descriptions in Habitat-Matterport scenes~\cite{P_R28}. SysNav reserves VLM calls for room-level decisions and leaves viewpoint selection and waypoint execution to geometric modules~\cite{P_E41}. Multimodal spatial language maps add image and audio features to an offline map, although a remembered sound marks where it was heard rather than its source~\cite{P_S12}. Temporal scene-object graphs report corner trapping from missing depth and episodes that end with the target out of view among their physical failures~\cite{P_S13}.

\subsection{Geographic Grounding, Priors, and Learned Actions}

Outdoor instructions often refer to places beyond the sensor horizon. OPEN associates visual semantics with OpenStreetMap and replans locally when road geometry differs from the map~\cite{P_M10}. G-DRAGON constrains generated identifiers to existing map entities before composing a behavior tree~\cite{P_E46}, whereas CAUSALNAV retrieves destinations from an object--building--cluster graph and combines global routing with constrained local control~\cite{P_E45}. HAM-Nav associates live landmarks with an imperfect hand-drawn map and predicts omitted ones before A*/TEB execution~\cite{P_S39}, whereas Mobility VLA retrieves a goal frame from a demonstration tour and routes over an offline graph without exploring~\cite{P_S43}.

Learned policies map language and images directly to motion, as in LeLaN, which trains a last-mile policy from labeled robot and action-free video~\cite{P_E50}. In 150 trials with 28 initially visible objects, its success within 0.2\,m is 0.89 versus 0.67 for OWL-v2 with ZoeDepth, while in a separate ablation collision training raises arrival with obstacles from 0.13 to 0.60 in 15 trials but lowers success without obstacles from 0.89 to 0.77. Uni-NaVid compresses multiscale video history before predicting discrete primitives whose vocabulary bounds the available actions~\cite{P_E49}, whereas LGX prompts a language model for exploration directions and falls back to random exploration after an invalid response~\cite{P_M20}.

\subsection{Candidate Selection and Behavioral Constraints}

VL-TGS generates LiDAR-based trajectories, projects them into an image for GPT-4V ranking, and follows the choice with DWA while later decisions are pending~\cite{P_F05}. However, its ranking cannot recover a route that the generator never proposes. Sem-NaVAE applies the same generate-and-select structure without a map, scoring conditional variational autoencoder (VAE) proposals with lightweight open-vocabulary segmentation and reporting 90\% success on 120--240\,m campus and park routes with few repetitions per route~\cite{P_R25}. VAMOS adds an embodiment filter, in which a per-robot affordance model trained in simulation re-ranks the image-space paths proposed by a VLM planner~\cite{P_R26}. With the planner unchanged, the affordance model of a wheeled robot raises its success on a ramp-or-stairs task from 6 to 9 of 10 runs.

Social and behavioral constraints separate preference from collision geometry at different levels of the stack. Cue-triggered judgments become social costs for DWA in VLM-Social-Nav~\cite{P_M07}, while NORM-Nav writes instruction-parsed semantic, directional, and speed layers into standard costmaps~\cite{P_R30}. VLM-informed path selection ranks clustered detours before an ORCA controller computes velocities~\cite{P_E43}, whereas GSON supplies group labels to a mid-level planner above a barrier-constrained predictive controller~\cite{P_F06}. OLiVia-Nav distills large-model social reasoning into lightweight encoders that are updated online, with evaluation against expert trajectories and personal-space violation rather than collision rates~\cite{P_R29}. Safe-VLN masks predicted waypoints with simulated occupancy and selects a new waypoint after a collision occurs~\cite{P_M18}. SocialNav-SUB shows that strong VLMs still trail human consensus and position-based rules on social scene questions~\cite{P_S44}, while VLM-GroNav revises terrain costs and waypoints from visual and proprioceptive exemplars~\cite{P_S40}. A grouping or grounding error can thus alter the route even when the local controller avoids detected people, whereas an occluded pedestrian can invalidate the controller itself.

\subsection{Memory, Timing, and Embodied Execution}

Semantic decisions remain valid for different durations. TARIC triangulates intermittent goal cues into a particle memory that keeps an executable goal direction during cue-free travel, although the memory drifts over long cue-free stretches~\cite{P_E42}. Other systems align the timing of reasoning with the scene, as SysNav queries at room transitions, GSON uses updated keyframes, and the social path selector associates asynchronous responses with current candidates~\cite{P_E41,P_F06,P_E43}. VAMOS plans at 1\,Hz on a laptop GPU~\cite{P_R26}, a rate that a fast local controller can bridge only while it retains a valid action. Embodiment then determines how a semantic choice becomes motion. VLFM uses different low-level controllers in simulation and on Spot~\cite{P_E47}, Uni-NaVid runs remote inference on a quadruped~\cite{P_E49}, and LeLaN executes on a wheeled base~\cite{P_E50}. For wheels, turning in place, lateral motion, and reversing are available only if the chassis supports them.

\subsection{Synthesis}
\label{sec:semantic-synthesis}

Candidate selection confines semantic errors more directly than cost layers, which keep geometry in charge of the final motion but can still misdirect the route, whereas direct policies depend on their history and action vocabulary. Semantic correctness and collision avoidance therefore require separate evaluation. As in Section~\ref{sec:planning}, nearly every study reports task outcomes, while fewer than a third report results under a named degradation condition (Table~\ref{tab:evaluation}).
