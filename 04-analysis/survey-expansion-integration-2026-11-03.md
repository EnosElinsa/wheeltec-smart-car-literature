## Changes to the comparative argument

- Localization distinguishes information selection, complementary sensing, numerical preconditioning, calibration, map validity and reference construction. Improved conditioning does not restore absent information.
- Planning compares direction-dependent terrain risk, expert-trained cost/speed/novelty maps, homotopy candidates and state/input constraints. Virtual risky branches, external pose, offboard control and numerical-only evidence remain explicit.
- Semantic navigation distinguishes multimodal memory, sketch/tour preparation, physical grounding, scene-understanding benchmarks and executed geometric control. Model fluency does not establish safe social state estimation.
- Collaboration separates lossy information selection from lossless coding, active observation scheduling, range geometry and conditional map merging. Reduced traffic and delayed overlap do not establish partition/reconnection safety.
- Recovery compares planner reinitialization, anti-novelty motion, depth-based trajectory correction, capability reassignment and online adaptation. Localization, resets, training servers, charging and human escalation remain part of deployment.
- Evaluation now explicitly discusses survivor-conditioned trajectory errors, missing timestamps, shared-map references, virtual hardware obstacles, uncertainty events and preparation/adaptation cost. Open challenges acknowledge implemented systems and focus on their unvalidated interfaces.
