# Historical survey evidence update: six themes as a reading framework

**Historical snapshot, October 25, 2026; translated during the November 2 rewrite.** This document preserves the analysis of 60 newly frozen expansion works. The historical record stated that official PDFs and raw BibTeX had been obtained and full-text checks completed for all 60; exact counts and acceptance records are in `99-archive/superseded/expansion-summary.json` (local historical snapshot). That historical completion wording does not mean that every current card has full verification. The current library contains **127 works, with 23 full and 104 light cards**; use the [integrated evidence analysis](integrated-evidence.md) for its updated scope and follow-up findings. The conclusions below cover this expansion snapshot and explicitly cited older-library evidence. Excerpts, physical PDF pages, and PDF/parsed-text hashes are recorded in `99-archive/superseded/expansion-evidence.csv` (local historical snapshot). The six themes are a reading framework, not a systematically validated map of the whole field.

The historical source expansion reached the full texts: 45 added journal papers covered 12 journals, with RA-L/T-RO accounting for 27/45; the other 18 added sources including RAS, IJRR, JFR, CEP, Autonomous Robots, and Science Robotics. This share was lower than the older main library's 30/45, but whitelist restrictions, abstract availability, and targeted selection still shaped it. It does not establish that source bias was removed or that field-wide retrieval was complete. Queues awaiting semantic review or metadata remained visible.

## T1: concrete degradation-adaptation mechanisms exist; changing platforms does not define novelty

The earlier judgment that degradation awareness and adaptive fusion merit investigation is strengthened, but the claim that ordinary fusion has not addressed degradation is untenable. LODESTAR uses reliable historical states and observation selection; TWO separates corrections explainable by wheel motion from orthogonal mismatch; MM-LINS handles severe degradation through map dormancy, dynamic restart, and revisit fusion. Comparisons should include these mechanisms rather than only a fixed-covariance EKF.

LODESTAR states on p. 8: “it fails when LiDAR returns are absent.” Its gains under short-term geometric degeneracy do not establish recovery from complete sensor loss; timings use an i9-13900K desktop CPU (p. 7). TWO states on p. 6 that the public data “provides no ToF camera at all” and substitutes the same LiDAR stream; self-collected ToF hardware data and public LiDAR playback should be reported separately. The EqF LiDAR–radar estimator states on p. 13 that “the choice of sensing modality plays the dominant role,” so modality gains cannot all be attributed to the filter formulation. DR-LRIO uses an aerial platform: retain its transferable decomposition of degradation, without counting it as wheeled-hardware validation.

For MM-LINS, the quantitative reference in the self-collected data is a common start/end position (p. 7), not full-trajectory ATE. Recovery requires revisits and map overlap (p. 9); the operating domain of that mechanism differs from continuously accurate localization. Where a table and a prose summary disagree, preserve the card's table-by-table audit rather than treating the most favorable sentence as the outcome for every sequence.

Related cards: [LODESTAR](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2026-LODESTAR-E01/review.md), [TWO](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2026-ToF-Wheel-E02/review.md), [MM-LINS](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2025-MM-LINS-E08/review.md), [EqF](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2026-Eq-LRIO-E06/review.md), [DR-LRIO](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2024-DR-LRIO-E09/review.md).

Adding DURAL, neural wheel kinematics, Screw degeneracy analysis, and Super Odometry broadens the comparators beyond fixed fusion to observation switching, online model learning, degeneracy-direction diagnosis, and layered adaptation. DURAL reports an APE of 0.167 m inside coverage and an RPE of 6.525% outside coverage in Table 4, p. 16, using only 15 total-station position points. The 6.456 cm map AC in Table 5, p. 17 follows blueprint alignment and 20 cm match filtering; it is not full-trajectory pose accuracy. Simulation and hardware thresholds differ, rotation degeneracy is ignored on hardware, and the wheel-speed nonholonomic constraint also requires near-zero sideslip.

Neural wheel kinematics requires adaptation in a feature-rich environment before degeneration, then freezes the network during degeneration (p. 4). It does not establish continued learning when an unknown terrain change and LiDAR degeneration occur together. The online full-frame time of 31 ms (Table 5, p. 11) is measured on a Ryzen 9/RTX 3060 Laptop, not a low-power CPU-only system. In the Screw paper, Table 2 on p. 29 reports orthogonal residuals in the degenerate subspace, not repaired-trajectory ATE. Degeneracy suppression is not implemented in its real KISS-ICP test (p. 36); real-data labels also come from statistics over 30 estimates of the same data. Diagnosis, suppression, and recovery require separate comparisons.

Super Odometry's 200 km/800 h is accumulated across heterogeneous platforms over six years (p. 2). The 2966 m/46 min combined-degradation test is one legged run (p. 3); wheeled smoke data come from manual driving with online estimation (p. 9). These cannot be described as 800 h of continuous full autonomy on one wheeled robot. The PDF includes a reference correction dated 2026-01-09 (p. 18), not another 2026 paper. The aggregation of average ATE in Table 2, p. 11 and the comparator behind the “second-best” improvement percentage in Table 1, p. 9 remain ambiguous. Preserve per-sequence values from the card rather than inferring universal superiority from the abstract.

## T2: long-term mapping and dynamic SLAM have close related work; separate evaluation units

The expansion compared long-term map maintenance, dynamic-object modeling, dynamic SLAM without GPU use, and joint point/Gaussian representations separately. Map cleaning, pose accuracy, dynamic-object trajectories, rendering quality, exploration completion time, and closed-loop task success are different quantities. They should not be collapsed into an overall claim of greater robustness. Per-paper conditions and numerical values are in the [theme directory](../01-papers/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/README.md) and `99-archive/superseded/expansion-comparison.csv` (local historical snapshot).

Long-term mapping papers require a distinction between offline processing of repeated real captures, online robot mapping, and continuous long-duration autonomous deployment. The historical full-text pass added UncertaintyMap and FIT-SLAM2 as related work on uncertainty frontiers and joint traversability/localizability exploration. DynoSAM and its earlier dynamic-SLAM version are one work family. FIT-SLAM2 is represented by the journal article, without counting its ICARA 2024 predecessor again.

Lifelong 3D Mapping reports “approximately 1.5 hours to process the whole KITTI 00 sequence with 4500 frames” on p. 6. Its 94.2% saving on p. 8 concerns multiversion map storage, not peak onboard RAM. ProbPer-LiLo uses real maps spanning two years, but requires pointwise semantics (p. 3) and assumes correct clustering (p. 5); a two-year span does not mean two years of continuous autonomous operation. NGD-SLAM states on p. 5 that YOLO's 30–40 ms is excluded from tracking time, so 60 FPS describes the tracking thread. PG-SLAM explicitly states that it “cannot achieve real-time performance” (p. 16). JPG-SLAM's rendering FPS likewise does not replace per-frame tracking/mapping time.

Related cards: [Lifelong](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2024-Lifelong-3D-Mapping-E11/review.md), [ProbPer-LiLo](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2026-ProbPer-LiLo-E16/review.md), [NGD-SLAM](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2025-NGD-SLAM-E19/review.md), [PG-SLAM](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2025-PG-SLAM-E17/review.md), [JPG-SLAM](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2025-JPG-SLAM-E20/review.md).

UncertaintyMap combines coverage and reconstruction uncertainty, but every experiment is a differential-drive Gazebo simulation (p. 7: “All experiments are performed with a differential robot simulated in Gazebo.”). Appendix p. 11 assumes known orientation and no association errors. PPS3 does not fully explore with incomplete landmarks (p. 8), so frontier exhaustion is not a universal complete-coverage guarantee. The 97% on p. 9 is correlation between UM and a landmark-uncertainty metric, not localization accuracy. The authors explicitly state on p. 6 that σmax is not a hard planning limit. Its mechanism is a candidate for a later Wheeltec comparison, without hardware evidence in this paper.

FIT-SLAM2's pure-exploration comparison in Table 2, p. 12 uses simulator ground-truth poses and reports 1809/880/342 s for three scenes. Tuned FAEL is faster in the corridor at 1706 s (p. 14: “FAEL marginally outperformed our method”). The roughly 33% gain comes from two unstructured simulations; it is not a hardware gain across all scenes. Table 4, p. 17 reports 63 ms with FI and 20.8 ms without FI; Table 3, p. 16 gives 620.58 ms for FIT-SLAM1. The prose claim of a 100-fold speedup therefore does not describe the FIT-SLAM1 ratio in that table, which is about 29.8. The hardware section explicitly says that “this experiment was not used to benchmark against other methods” (p. 19); it is a proof of concept. Roadmap aging or disconnection in dynamic environments remains future work. Fisher-information guidance away from poorly observable regions is also distinct from a general safety guarantee.

Related cards: [UncertaintyMap](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2025-Active-SLAM-Uncertainty-E12/review.md), [FIT-SLAM2](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2025-FIT-SLAM2-E13/review.md).

## T3: hardware safety methods exist; guarantees depend on models and evidence conditions

Replace the earlier statement that learning-based control lacks safety guarantees with a more precise one: CBF/HOCBF, risk measures, and safe-learning mechanisms already exist, and their initial-state, noise, observability, input-limit, solver-feasibility, and control-period conditions must be checked. A theorem or a successful trajectory does not imply zero collisions in arbitrary environments.

In CVaR-BF's IROS simulation with five obstacles and σ=.05, the p. 7 table row “Proposed 0.917 1.000 0.058 13.036 13.092” has CR=.058. Its conditional theory does not mean zero collisions throughout that simulation, and no hardware statistics are reported. The cloud-vehicle MPC statement on p. 9, “reduces the collision rate by 32% to 77%,” is a relative reduction in simulation. Its small Ackermann hardware uses optical localization and virtual moving obstacles, not autonomous onboard perception in unknown outdoor scenes.

The online LiDAR GP-CBF does include a three-TurtleBot experiment (p. 7), so a claim that CBF methods are simulation-only is also wrong. Retain its remote-compute and Vicon conditions. Seeing Danger is compared experimentally with NoRisk, Reactive, and the instantaneous-probability planner; RAMP/EVORA appear in the paper's positioning table and should not be represented as matched experimental baselines.

Related cards: [CVaR-BF](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2025-CVaR-CBF-E27/review.md), [Cloud-vehicle MPC](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2026-Cloud-MPC-UGV-E21/review.md), [LiDAR GP-CBF](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2024-LiDAR-CBF-E26/review.md), [Seeing Danger](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2026-Env-Risk-Learning-E28/review.md).

The later addition, Circulation-CBF, directly addresses non-goal equilibria and repeated circulation near nonconvex obstacles; the four-TurtleBot3 position-swap test is physical hardware (p. 11). Concrete related work on deadlock recovery therefore exists. Its memory rule for sustained circulation and input constraints are the incremental contribution, rather than a reinvention of its predecessor's LiDAR-GP safety function. Computation runs on a “remote PC equipped with an Intel i7 processor” (p. 9). The 0.485 ms mean on p. 10 covers parameter calculation and the QP, excluding the perception/network chain. Retain the Vicon, low-speed dead-zone, and planar single-integrator-mapping limits.

SH-MPC certifies joint open-loop collision probability over the predicted trajectory. The authors state on p. 14 that the “guarantees provided in this paper rely on an accurate model of the uncertainty.” The .0092/.0107 on pp. 11–12 are Monte Carlo estimates of maximum planned risk, not observed collision rates. The 27 ms mean and 66/67 ms maxima come from an eight-pedestrian simulation; the maxima exceed the 20 Hz control period. Jackal hardware uses motion-capture pose and up to three pedestrians; the CARLA bicycle model is a separate simulation. They cannot be combined into real autonomous-driving safety statistics. The finite horizon, predicted distribution, feasibility, and zero-slack conditions must appear in comparisons.

Related cards: [Circulation-CBF](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2025-CECBF-E22/review.md), [SH-MPC](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2025-Scenario-Safe-Planning-E25/review.md).

## T4: specify communication limits through messages, outages, and convergence conditions

Decentralized SLAM, semantic compression, relative localization, coverage planning, and intermittent rendezvous address different bottlenecks. The broad topic of communication-limited multi-robot systems is not itself an unresolved problem. For each paper, the comparison matrix records communication assumptions, whether partitions are allowed, information required after recovery, and measured map-consistency, localization-accuracy, or task-coverage evidence.

The swapped older citations for X08 ROAM and X13 fault-tolerant formation have been corrected. X08 concerns local planning during communication interruption; X13 is a numerical study of predefined-time fault-tolerant formation. The latter cannot supply statistics on fault propagation or reconnection recovery in a real network. Full-text checking of the RAS semantic-rendezvous paper confirmed that it extends its own references [19,20]: AIxIA 2023 and IROS 2024 10801321 (p. 3). Reference [30] on p. 14 is da Silva et al.'s IROS 10802343, selected in this expansion, with different authors and a different work family. The journal article adds semantic high-connectivity-area U/A policies, joint exploration-area analysis, and expanded simulation; count the journal representative once. Similar titles alone do not establish duplication, and its own conference predecessors should not be counted again.

Related records: [complete T4 directory](../01-papers/T4-multi-robot-coordination/README.md), plus the supplemental full-text rendezvous-family audit in `../99-archive/history-2026-10-25.zip` (local only). The earlier metadata audit is preserved; the supplemental full-text decision is the applicable historical version-family decision.

The RAS semantic-rendezvous study has more than 400 ROS/Gazebo simulations, with semantic-oracle and ideal-formation assumptions, and excludes permanent navigation failures without reporting their number. Hardware remains future work (p. 13), so this is not physical wireless-network evidence. For eight robots, BT is slower than FBE by 4.44%/4.63% in Ring/Office (p. 12), which does not support speed gains at every scale. Semantic bias can shorten rendezvous time while sacrificing explored area or producing repeated detours (Fig. 14, p. 13). Report rendezvous, coverage, and failure filtering separately.

Commerge includes real wireless data collection, but its core is central-server scan selection and map merging, not a complete autonomous-exploration system under partitions. The 73.3% on p. 27 corresponds to robot β sending 5773 and receiving 1541 descriptor/keyframe messages. It is not a configured packet-loss rate, task-success rate, or universal loss-tolerance bound. The approximately 7000 MB → 1.3 MB in Fig. 2, p. 6 is a selected-submap example; the cumulative Town mean on p. 23 is 13.7 MB. The authors state on p. 32 that large-scale real-time operation on IPC/Nano remains difficult; some public-data evaluations use ground-truth trajectories. Compare selective data compression separately from end-to-end onboard autonomy.

Related cards: [Commerge](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Commerge-E36/review.md), [Semantic rendezvous](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2025-Semantic-Rendezvous-E38/review.md).

WiSER-X further shows that no continuous map communication does not mean no communication or prior information. Its setup needs a known region boundary, initial global positions, and receivable pings. UP-Squared handles onboard perception/SLAM, while exploration decisions run offboard. Simulated coverage is approximately 93% (p. 7), not completion of every region. Differences between the text and caption in reported site area remain in the [card](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-WiSER-X-E32/review.md); do not select one value as a universal reference.

iMESA directly limits the claim that decentralization has solved outage recovery. Its initiated two-stage communication must complete, and incorrect loop closures are removed from the real pose graphs beforehand (p. 9); it explicitly “does not guarantee convergence” (p. 6). The real-data experiment is Nebula pose-graph playback (p. 9). In a separate synthetic long-duration test with up to 10,000 poses, the mean iteration is below 40 ms and excludes network transport (p. 8). Parallel Graph Inference's 11.5/6.9 ms step times are offline i7 computation (p. 7); UTIAS also uses a beacon vehicle with landmark observations, and old measurements offer little gain under an 8 s delay (p. 6). IROS intermittent rendezvous is simulated: its two Gazebo validations use predefined K/W plans (p. 8), and rescheduling still requires common information (p. 6). Existing methods therefore cover several sparse-communication mechanisms. A comparison should target a specific combination of loss during protocol execution, resynchronization, and closed-loop task outcomes.

Related cards: [iMESA](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2024-iMESA-E40/review.md), [Parallel Graph](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Async-Graph-Localization-E37/review.md), [Intermittent rendezvous](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2024-Intermittent-Rendezvous-E39/review.md).

SlideSLAM does include onboard autonomous exploration by three wheeled robots. However, its intermittent-communication statistics on p. 15 come from laptop ROS playback, publishing the full database every 10 seconds. The 0.304–0.309 MB in Table VI is message size, not MB/s. Multi-SLAM+LPFE includes four/eight physical wheeled robots and reports 8 KB/s per robot for map-merging data (p. 7). Local continuation during disconnection and merging after reconnection are described qualitatively, without long-outage duration or recovery-error statistics. Both are direct wheeled related work, with communication boundaries that still require precise verification.

4CNet explicitly depends on global GPS localization. Prediction averages 5.452 s on an RTX 4090 (p. 13); this is not onboard latency on the hardware system's GTX 1050. DMT-CPP explicitly uses a central planning entity (p. 5) and OptiTrack on hardware. The 24.54/33.62 ms on p. 6 covers one coverage-stage step, following preparation times of 10.99/15.06 s. Three-robot hardware coverage is 98.7% (p. 7). The title does not imply distributed communication robustness or 100% coverage in every test.

Related cards: [SlideSLAM](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2025-SlideSLAM-E31/review.md), [Multi-SLAM+LPFE](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Multi-SLAM-LPFE-E34/review.md), [4CNet](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-4CNet-E33/review.md), [DMT-CPP](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-DMT-CPP-E35/review.md).

## T5: language-navigation gains do not replace avoidance, compute, or platform evidence

LeLaN supplies direct wheeled-platform evidence, but its main task is final approach to a target visible at the initial pose (p. 3). The 89% versus 67% for the strongest baseline on p. 6 uses a 0.2 m arrival threshold and reimplemented baselines. Adding avoidance supervision in Table 3, p. 7 raises arrival with obstacles from .13 to .60 while lowering obstacle-free arrival from .89 to .77. The authors acknowledge that “reliable collision avoidance with precise language-conditioned navigation is a remaining challenge” (p. 19). Long-range demonstrations also require prior teleoperation to collect topological images (p. 22), rather than prior-free long-range exploration.

The 17-page Uni-NaVid main paper was checked through offline OCR and page images in the historical pass. Remote A100 inference and quadruped demonstrations are not wheeled deployment on a Jetson. Predicting four discrete actions in 0.2 s is not a complete 5 Hz feedback loop. Main quantitative results are simulated; hardware statistics refer to supplementary material that was not delivered with the main paper. Video-memory compression may transfer, but wheel-compatible actions, camera height, communication delay, and low-level avoidance require separate validation.

Related cards: [LeLaN](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2025-LeLaN-E50/review.md), [Uni-NaVid](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2025-Uni-NaVid-E49/review.md). Dynamic-map, goal-reasoning, and social-evaluation conditions for other T5 papers are in the [per-paper directory](../01-papers/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/README.md); do not rank results directly across datasets.

Direct onboard-inference evidence also exists. LeLaN runs its policy on Orin AGX (p. 19), with 0.054 s per action in Table 2, p. 6; that is action-inference time. SysNav has quantitative mecanum-wheel hardware evidence but relies on 3D point clouds, a panoramic camera, an RTX 4090 laptop, and cloud Gemini (pp. 5–7). Its separation of room-level semantics from low-level autonomy is informative; ordinary 2D LiDAR and USB-camera inputs are not equivalent. TARIC also tests a wheeled Scout Mini, with an aggregate 40% success over 40 real episodes (p. 7). Its roughly 2.8 s semantic updates on an external RTX 3090 and 50 Hz low-level control require separate reporting; dynamic pedestrians remain an explicit cause of takeover (p. 8). These papers strengthen the mechanism case for layered semantic/geometric processing while requiring platform transfer, onboard inference, and closed-loop safety to be checked individually.

Related cards: [SysNav](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2026-SysNav-E41/review.md), [TARIC](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2026-TARIC-E42/review.md).

Open-vocabulary maps also require comparison by task prerequisites. VLFM assigns semantic scores to exploration frontiers in an unknown single-floor environment. Its zero-shot claim concerns the ObjectNav semantic layer; the low-level PointNav policy still trains for 2.5 billion steps over about seven days (p. 4). Spot hardware is qualitative evidence using a 4090 Mobile laptop (p. 6). HOV-SG first constructs a static floor–room–object graph and then queries it hierarchically. Real Spot object retrieval is 29/41 and navigation is 23/41 (p. 10), with different success definitions. The paper explicitly calls its system “unsuitable for real-time mapping” (p. 11). Its 75% representation-storage saving is relative to VLMaps, not runtime memory or dynamic-update gains. These works address different problems; the open-vocabulary label alone does not justify a performance ordering.

Related cards: [VLFM](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2024-VLFM-E47/review.md), [HOV-SG](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2024-HOV-SG-E48/review.md).

Outdoor language navigation already has wheeled closed-loop examples, each with specific conditions. CAUSALNAV builds its initial graph through exploration of the whole environment and defines success as being within 10 m of the target (p. 6); long-term autonomous exploration remains future work (p. 8). It cannot be compared directly with LeLaN's 0.2 m final-approach rate. G-DRAGON can execute offline, but startup still requires an edge server to generate a behavior tree (p. 8); four hardware scenes do not provide large-sample reliability statistics. The WheelTec label in Fig. 4 belongs to a GPS peripheral; the chassis is a Scout Mini, so it is not evidence of validated WHEELTEC-chassis transfer.

Related cards: [CAUSALNAV](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2026-CAUSALNAV-E45/review.md), [G-DRAGON](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2026-G-DRAGON-E46/review.md).

OpenIN adds instance-memory updates after everyday objects move, but needs an initial CRSG (p. 4); its dynamic setting mainly involves displaced objects and furniture. In scene 5 of Table II, the fifth target's individual SR is 60%, while the fraction completing all five targets consecutively is zero (p. 6). Single-step detection does not replace whole-sequence task reliability. The real wheeled Autolabor case uses move_base (p. 8), without a hardware task-success rate or inference latency. From Obstacles to Etiquette tests four scenes on Spot with three trials each (p. 6). Its onboard Orin processing explicitly excludes the VLM, which is hosted by a networked H100 service. Zero social-zone intrusion in those controlled scenes supports that path metric under those test conditions, not closed-loop safety in arbitrary crowds.

Related cards: [OpenIN](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2025-OpenIN-E44/review.md), [From Obstacles to Etiquette](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2026-VLM-Social-Path-E43/review.md).

## T6: detection, fault tolerance, and recovery exist; joint evaluation must be specified

Withdraw the claim that recovery has not been studied. Related work exists on actuator tolerance, fault-tolerant localization, GP recovery, fault-aware CBF control, and reasoning-driven fallback. Determine whether their detected condition, trigger, recovery action, and task evaluation match the proposed chain.

The visual anomaly CP paper reports AUC .8174 with a 95% interval [.7995,.8341] on p. 6. At α=.05, Table III on p. 6 gives recall .3097 and FNR .6903; performance discussion is on p. 7. Control of false alarms on normal data is not hazardous-event detection or a collision-safety guarantee, and the single-hallway deployment does not test closed-loop avoidance. The 58 minutes in charging planning (p. 7) is aggregate work by three robots within a 36-minute cycle, not single-robot endurance. The paper is simulated evidence, not multiday physical charging operation.

Related cards: [CP anomaly detection](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-Conformal-Anomaly-E54/review.md), [Charging planning](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-Persistent-Charging-E58/review.md), [GP recovery](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2024-GP-Navigation-Recovery-E59/review.md). Every fault-safety theorem retains its model, observation, and initial-state conditions. The historical full-text audit was not an independent re-proof of all mathematics.

FORTRESS is a concrete existing fallback planner for out-of-distribution risk, dependent on an external runtime monitor. It does not specifically validate detection of, and recovery from, erroneous foundation-model outputs. ANYmal evidence concerns semantic classification; the hardware that executes closed-loop fallback is a quadrotor (Appendix F.2, pp. 24–25). Table 2, p. 8 reports about 11 ms for reasoning and about 1.28 s for reach-avoid planning. The greater-than-90% safe-plan success in Appendix D.3, p. 23 belongs to CARLA rooftop landing. They cannot be combined into a claim that ANYmal achieved more than 90% real recovery at millisecond latency, or directly applied to a wheeled feedback loop. The transferable contribution separates low-frequency model queries/calibration caches from online dynamics-constrained fallback; wheeled models and the detection chain require separate validation. See the [full-text card](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-FORTRESS-E60/review.md).

KSFD adds kinematic-consistency diagnosis without a vehicle-dynamics model, but its real-time claim refers to causal computation. The phrase “without the need for future data” on p. 7 does not mean a measured onboard deadline. The real ground/aerial platforms only supply logs to which artificial faults are later added. Experimental position substitutes for radar, so neither real radar nor closed-loop takeover after a fault is tested (pp. 9, 11). Multiple-fault isolation is unsupported, and statistical threshold calibration remains future work. This counters the assertion that training-free fault detection has no close related work, but does not establish natural-failure detection rates or safe-recovery statistics in this library.

mEclares combines an information-decay field, energy/return-interval constraints, and return scheduling after central-node failure. A real quadrotor and mobile ground station support hardware feasibility. However, p. 15 specifies “instantaneous recharging,” with T_ch=0 in all experiments. The word persistent therefore does not establish physical charging, aging, or multiday energy-loop validation. The three-robot total delay is 600±150 ms (p. 20); 30 robots are simulated, and the real state depends on Vicon. Safe return after central failure differs from continued task execution. Return-energy and slot feasibility also do not constitute a complete collision-avoidance proof.

Related cards: [KSFD](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-KSFD-E51/review.md), [mEclares](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-mEclares-E57/review.md).

## Conclusions supported and unsupported by this historical snapshot

Supported: the reviewed papers provide multiple mechanisms for degradation adaptation, long-term mapping, safe control, collaboration, and semantic navigation. Experimental conditions, sensing, and compute cost materially change their transfer domain. Wheeled closed-loop, real-data playback, and simulation evidence require separate descriptions.

Unsupported: the six classes exhaust the field; failure to encounter a work in this library means nobody studies it; desktop or remote-GPU time establishes R680 onboard real time; a conditional safety theorem is a zero-collision statistic; or changing to ROS 2/Nav2 or WHEELTEC is itself a new contribution.
