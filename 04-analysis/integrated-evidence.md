## localization-mapping: 38 library works

The common estimation problem is deciding which constraints remain informative when observations, motion models, or prior maps become unreliable. [M39](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2022-slip-aware-fusion-M39/review.md) estimates longitudinal slip and speed from wheel/inertial relations; this is an online estimation contribution, without a demonstrated navigator consuming the improved estimate. [E01](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2026-LODESTAR-E01/review.md) uses selected well-conditioned past states and direction-aware point contributions to constrain LiDAR-inertial estimation, but missing LiDAR returns remain outside its scope. [E02](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2026-ToF-Wheel-E02/review.md) instead couples limited ToF measurements to wheel odometry on a small wheeled platform. These are complementary uses of redundancy, not interchangeable solutions to every sensing outage.

Representations and evaluation scale also matter. [E10](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2025-PL-VIWO-E10/review.md) fuses point, line, inertial, and wheel constraints on vehicle data, while [E19](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2025-NGD-SLAM-E19/review.md) separates dynamic-object handling from ordinary geometric tracking without using its installed GPU. [E15](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2026-DynoSAM-E15/review.md) uses object-motion smoothing and a full-batch back end, so its estimation results do not establish online navigation. The desktop/public-dataset setting of these examples must remain distinct from an onboard feedback loop.

The follow-up narrows the interface to fault handling. [F04](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2024-Switch-SLAM-F04/review.md) changes estimator initialization and optimization when LiDAR/visual status changes. [F03](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Fault-Map-Update-F03/review.md) isolates observations or map faults and permits landmark correction through covariance inflation under redundancy and fault-count assumptions. Its reset/warning response for odometry divergence is specified; an executed reset or navigation maneuver is not demonstrated. [F11](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2026-M2UD-F11/review.md) supplies M2UD's uneven-terrain localization/mapping resource, with RTK- and SLAM-dependent references and a separate handheld mapping subset. Together these sources support estimator response and benchmark selection, while leaving a common estimate-to-navigation recovery trial to design.

[N01](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2026-AESF-LIO-N01/review.md) and [N02](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2023-SE2-LIO-N02/review.md) distinguish residual weighting from a near-planar motion prior; neither supplies a new independent sensing channel. [N03](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2025-Trace-Slip-N03/review.md) requires preserved lug traces on suitable soil. [N19](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2024-Active-Collision-N19/review.md) uses known-map contact observations, with manually driven hardware contacts and a separately simulated active planner. [N20](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2024-DMSA-N20/review.md) broadens the dense-estimation comparison while retaining slower-than-recording desktop processing and sensor-compatibility limits.

## traversability-planning-control: 42 library works

A traversability representation becomes operational when a planner or controller consumes it under a stated vehicle model. [E13](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2025-FIT-SLAM2-E13/review.md) joins geometric terrain risk, frontier exploration, and landmark-based localizability, while separating perfect-pose planner comparisons from a SLAM self-ablation and qualitative rover evidence. [F09](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Terrain-Voronoi-F09/review.md), primarily multi-robot, supplies direct Ackermann terrain-navigation evidence through plane fitting, terrain classification, Voronoi allocation, and subgoal selection. The covariance used for that plane fit is scatter in local terrain points, not robot-pose covariance. Terrain-aware route choice is therefore established; the specific coupling from calibrated pose uncertainty to terrain cost or speed remains unestablished in the selected cards.

Safety filters have different contracts from terrain classification. [E26](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2024-LiDAR-CBF-E26/review.md) constructs a LiDAR-derived barrier and changes physical robot commands, with external pose and remote computation. Its proof requires suitable state, input, and update conditions. [E22](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2025-CECBF-E22/review.md) adds circulation to address barrier-induced stagnation under its model. [E25](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2025-Scenario-Safe-Planning-E25/review.md) uses scenario optimization to certify an accepted plan against a predicted obstacle distribution, while plain deceleration is a fallback rather than a separately proved safe policy. [F08](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2025-Perceive-Confidence-F08/review.md) combines environment-level occupancy calibration with a safe planner on a quadruped, adding a perception-to-action assurance mechanism under known-state and static-scene assumptions.

[F10](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2026-Multi-Fidelity-GP-F10/review.md) is relevant boundary evidence: localization uncertainty changes field reconstruction and trajectories in physical aquatic exploration. It should be retained as an existing uncertainty-to-action ingredient, without treating a tank light field as wheeled terrain or external cameras as onboard localization. The remaining experimental question is how belief quality changes footprint inflation, admissible terrain, or braking limits on a ground robot, with error and task outcomes measured together. The three context works in this module locate historical tracking, reinforcement-learning review, and social-navigation ideas; their inclusion does not add independent empirical evidence for the new interfaces.

[N05](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2024-SLAM-Aware-Exploration-N05/review.md) schedules loop closures over a supplied prior graph, with a common Karto back-end but unequal prior requirements across some comparators. [N08](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2026-Anisotropic-Exploration-N08/review.md) uses an anisotropic quasi-static terrain cost rather than calibrated rollover risk. [N12](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2026-Spatiotemporal-Tubes-N12/review.md) conditions tube-based reach-avoid control on its dynamics and terminal separation assumptions, with explicit input constraints left for future work. [N14](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2026-Resilient-Event-MPC-N14/review.md) uses industrial-PC HIL control; fewer optimization calls do not establish proportional energy savings.

## semantic-language-navigation: 18 library works

Semantic systems differ in what a model is allowed to select. [E47](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2024-VLFM-E47/review.md) uses language-grounded value information to guide exploration, with simulator benchmarks and qualitative Spot evidence. [E50](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2025-LeLaN-E50/review.md) learns a language-conditioned final approach on a wheeled platform; a visible target and its avoidance limits define a narrower task than general semantic exploration. [E44](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2025-OpenIN-E44/review.md) updates instance-level carrier/object memory and uses a fixed search policy with hosted model queries. Its staged moved-object setting differs from avoiding people who move during a run. These task distinctions prevent treating their success quantities as a common performance league.

The new cards already contain safety integration. In [F05](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2025-VL-TGS-F05/review.md), a hosted model selects among geometry-derived candidate trajectories while DWA continues along a selected route during model delay. In [F06](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2025-GSON-F06/review.md), model-derived group labels alter mid-level routing, and a local NMPC-CBF controller uses tracked pedestrian states. It would be inaccurate to say that semantic navigation lacks classical constraints. The narrower question is whether late or incorrect semantics, missed tracks, query cost, and model unavailability are evaluated with an explicit fallback and paired collision/task outcomes.

Boundary mechanisms clarify what a combined test needs. [F07](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-Sentinel-F07/review.md) separates fast policy inconsistency from slow progress monitoring but evaluates detection rather than navigation recovery. [F08](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2025-Perceive-Confidence-F08/review.md) conditions safe planning on calibrated occupancy estimates without language reasoning. [E60](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-FORTRESS-E60/review.md) precomputes semantic fallback candidates and executes reach-avoid planning on a quadrotor under an external alarm. Their timing and assurance contracts are useful interfaces, not a demonstrated single wheeled system with unrestricted model reasoning and guaranteed collision freedom.

[N09](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2026-Co-NavGPT-N09/review.md) integrates semantic frontier allocation with geometric goal execution. HM3D provides quantitative comparisons, while quadrupeds provide separate demonstrations with workstation perception/planning. The result narrows questions about delayed shared-map assignments without establishing wheeled deployment or communication-loss tolerance.

## multi-robot: 24 library works

Coordination couples state quality to who receives which information. [E31](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2025-SlideSLAM-E31/review.md) shares sparse semantic observations and supports onboard wheeled exploration, but its communication measurements include scheduled laptop replay rather than controlled physical outages. [E36](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Commerge-E36/review.md) benchmarks map-merging server resources, so server latency does not establish an onboard team deadline. [E40](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2024-iMESA-E40/review.md) studies distributed optimization with simulated communication and cleaned graph inputs; it should not be interpreted as general recovery from corrupt loop closures. [F15](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2025-CoVOR-SLAM-F15/review.md) exchanges poses, covariances, and ranges to reduce visual map exchange, using rover recordings and virtual agents rather than a live multi-rover outage trial.

Responses to degraded communication are already diverse. [F12](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Failure-Tracking-F12/review.md) changes tracking modes for sensing or communication failures and can merge a target estimate after temporary disconnection, on an aerial platform with connectivity assumptions. [F13](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-C-CBF-F13/review.md) imposes channel- and collision-related constraints in network-in-the-loop simulation, with reception models and barrier slack affecting the guarantee. [F14](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Cross-Rank-F14/review.md) uses position histories during physical legged exploration with intermittent messages and a shared frame. Receiving intermittent packets differs from sustaining a complete partition.

[F03](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Fault-Map-Update-F03/review.md) adds correlation-aware fusion and conditional map-fault repair; it assumes common landmarks, timing, and sufficiently redundant observations, and KLA requires consistent incoming estimates. [F09](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Terrain-Voronoi-F09/review.md) demonstrates terrain-aware motion by physical wheeled teammates under a common-LAN map-sharing architecture. Neither a communication radius nor changing collaboration windows constitutes a measured partition/reunion protocol. A joint ground-team test should include stale and wrong shared state, local task continuation, merge consistency, fault spread, and safety after reconnection. This is a bounded evaluation proposal, not a claim that no other literature studies resilience.

[N07](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2024-Active-Graph-Exploration-N07/review.md) extends [N05](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2024-SLAM-Aware-Exploration-N05/review.md) to simulated team graphs; the related publications are not independent experimental corroboration. [N11](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2025-Range-Integrity-N11/review.md) formulates distributed range-consistency monitoring but evaluates a centralized mixed-reality implementation with emulated ranges. Approximation bounds, algorithm distribution, measurement source and actual placement need separate interpretation.

## fault-recovery-deployment: 25 library works

The primary cards separate detection, tolerance, integrity, and response. [E51](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-KSFD-E51/review.md) diagnoses from kinematic consistency on recordings with injected faults. [M15](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2024-FT-PPC-WMR-M15/review.md) and [M01](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2026-FT-UGV-tracking-M01/review.md) maintain prescribed tracking envelopes in numerical models without explicit diagnosis; the latter assumes perfect position. [E52](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-Adaptive-Fault-Control-E52/review.md) adds physical tracking under partial actuator faults and saturation. [F02](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-Event-FTC-F02/review.md) reconstructs modeled faults and event-triggers tracking in a networked hardware-in-loop setup. These contributions already address fault tolerance, but an error envelope is not a collision safe set and a tolerated feedback fault is not recovery of a failed localization pipeline.

[M35](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2023-reliable-MCL-M35/review.md) combines reliability estimation with continuous estimator relocalization. [M28](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2024-PF-integrity-M28/review.md) and [X07](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2025-EKF-SLAM-integrity-X07/review.md) bound hazardous undetected error under estimator-specific association and fault assumptions. [F01](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2025-Line-Integrity-F01/review.md) evaluates protection-level error coverage while reserving operational alert thresholds for future use. [E54](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-Conformal-Anomaly-E54/review.md) calibrates nominal anomaly false alarms; [F07](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-Sentinel-F07/review.md) combines policy-consistency and progress detectors. Their outputs cannot be substituted for one another without changing the event, conditioning, or population being measured.

Executed responses exist elsewhere in this primary module. [E59](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2024-GP-Navigation-Recovery-E59/review.md) stops and moves toward a state with lower predicted planner-failure risk, with simulation controls and staged ground/legged demonstrations. [E55](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-Fault-Attack-CBF-E55/review.md) uses fault-hypothesis-aware barrier control in numerical models, whereas [E60](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-FORTRESS-E60/review.md) supplies semantic fallback planning on an aerial platform. [M11](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-CODEI-M11/review.md) treats resource selection as task-dependent co-design; [E58](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-Persistent-Charging-E58/review.md) schedules periodic fleet charging under known-duration assumptions; [M37](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2022-long-term-supervision-M37/review.md) exposes human assistance, cloud dependence, idle-time events, and docking during long-duration assistive operation. The synthesis is a response contract: state what remains trusted, what alarm means, which action executes, when it completes, and how safety and useful service are both assessed.

[N10](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2026-NMPC-Safe-Learning-N10/review.md) implements tracking-loss stopping and relocalization, with restarted trials after failed recovery; [N17](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2023-BT-Recovery-N17/review.md) executes hybrid-robot recovery and retains a human-assistance boundary. [N18](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2022-PAAD-N18/review.md) supplies predictive monitoring and rosbag timing without an autonomous response loop. [N13](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-Predictive-Swarm-Faults-N13/review.md) evaluates simulated withdrawal with assumed maintenance, while [N15](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2025-Resilient-STL-N15/review.md) permits explicit mission relaxation. [N04](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2026-LIO-Reproducibility-N04/review.md) distinguishes implementation repeatability from physical mission variability, and [N06](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2024-Predictive-Data-N06/review.md) assesses representation maintenance on recorded revisits rather than uninterrupted service.

## Cross-module evaluation contract

Error, task outcome, designed degradation, and integrity/reliability are overlapping dimensions. Designed degradation is an experimental factor, not a higher historical stage or a performance score. A detector false-positive guarantee, a predicted collision bound, and a field success rate have different events and denominators. The minimum useful joint trial logs degradation onset, estimator/monitor output, threshold decision, executed response, delay, and task outcome together. It retains no-fault controls, a simple response baseline, failed/aborted attempts, source/reference dependence, calibration conditions, data age, and compute placement.

F03 establishes observation exclusion and conditional landmark repair; its specified reset/warning is not an executed navigation recovery. F09 establishes a physical wheeled terrain loop; its point-set covariance is not pose uncertainty. F11 contributes benchmark recordings with mixed RTK/SLAM reference dependencies and a handheld mapping subset; a compute-sensitive trajectory score is not end-to-end control latency. Those distinctions change the proposed experiments without erasing the existing contributions.

## Reproducible analytic gap priority

The four rows in [gap-priority.csv](../06-data/gap-priority.csv) are **analytic judgments for this project**, not empirical measurements, literature-frequency scores, or rankings validated by the cited authors. They preserve G1--G4 as corpus-scoped integration questions. Card counts do not enter the ranking.

Use the following lexicographic order, with lower final rank meaning earlier experimental priority:

1. Sort `evidence_maturity` descending. Grade 3 means the exact joint behavior already has a wheeled closed-loop test with paired safety/task outcomes in the linked cards; grade 2 means a wheeled closed-loop component chain exists together with empirical complementary ingredients, but their proposed joint test is unestablished; grade 1 means the distinctive degraded mode is supported mainly by replay, simulation, or boundary platforms, despite other wheeled components; grade 0 means only analytic ingredients. A grade 3 item would normally be reframed as replication rather than an open integration question.
2. Sort `unvalidated_interface_count` ascending. Count the distinct decision contracts listed in `unvalidated_interfaces`, rather than software packages or papers. A monitor-to-supervisor safe-action contract is one interface; a query-age/timeout contract is separate from semantic-to-constraint coupling; pose-belief-to-terrain-cost and cost-to-action-limit are separate. For partition studies, local-mode control, reunion fusion, and corrupt-state containment are separate. The declared list fixes the granularity used here.
3. Sort `test_feasibility_grade` ascending. Grade 1 denotes a controlled single-ground-robot indoor trial with repeatable signal or query-delay injections; grade 2 adds uneven-terrain facilities and independent pose/terrain reference; grade 3 requires a moving multi-robot team, controlled network faults, and shared-state reconciliation. These are planned protocol requirements, not claims that R680 hardware or facilities are already available. No grade is a measured cost.

Exact equal tuples retain the same rank. If display order among tied rows is needed, sort by gap ID without changing their rank. The current rows yield G1, G2, G3, G4 because maturity is tied for G1--G3, G1 has a smaller interface count, and the controlled indoor G2 test has fewer declared facility requirements than G3. G4 follows because its distinctive partition/reunion/fault mode is not established in a wheeled closed-loop task in the selected cards. Changing any input or protocol assumption requires recording the new row and rerunning the tuple sort.

G2 acknowledges existing semantic safety integration in F06, calibrated perception/planning in F08, and semantic fallback in E60. G3 acknowledges the actual wheeled terrain loop in F09. G4 acknowledges F03's conditional map repair and correlation handling as candidate baselines, without treating them as a completed sustained-partition task test. G1 acknowledges estimator relocalization, switching, repair, tolerant tracking, and executed planner recovery as distinct existing links. None of the residual questions implies a field-wide absence or scientific impossibility.
