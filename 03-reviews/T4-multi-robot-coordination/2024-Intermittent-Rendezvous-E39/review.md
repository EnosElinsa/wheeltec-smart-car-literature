## Problem
In multi-robot exploration of unknown areas, staying connected or returning to a base station to report limits coverage efficiency. The robots must schedule the time between dispersed exploration and intermittent rendezvous for map exchange.
## Mechanism
Exploration reward is formulated as a DEC-POMDP, but the robots use a finite-state local controller with a greedy frontier utility (new area over path cost) and do not solve the DEC-POMDP optimally. The sub-team rendezvous relations K and exploration durations W are cast as a Job Shop Scheduling Problem (JSSP) instance, and a genetic algorithm minimizes area-estimate error, waiting, makespan, and connectivity metrics. After a rendezvous, the robots reselect frontier cluster positions; OursSync replans with common information after full-team meetings. The initial plan is generated by one robot from the estimated bounding-rectangle area of the mission zone and sensing and travel speeds, and is then distributed.
## Baselines and controls
- BS: a restricted static base station; robots periodically return to exchange maps.
- CRN1R: a restricted base station plus one relay per robot.
- CRN2R: two relays per robot.
- Ablation: Ours with a fixed initial plan versus OursSync with about four full-team synchronized replans.
These are heuristic baselines designed by the authors from earlier work, not full reproductions of the original methods.
## Platform and setup
- Discrete benchmark: 20 satellite maps (cities, parking lots, airports, construction sites from Google Maps) with precomputed cell decomposition; 4 robots, 10 runs per scene from random start clusters, at most 50000 discrete steps. View 5 cells, robot communication diameter 4 cells, all robots start in the same cluster; base-station and relay range 12 cells, and CRN robots can deploy relays after 2000 steps.
- Gazebo and ROS proof of concept: four robots, two trials, with predetermined K and W matrices to test the rendezvous execution subsystem; the genetic-algorithm plan is not generated online. Mean speed 0.5 m/s, simulated communication range 3 m.
- No physical robot experiment.
## Evidence type and transfer
DEC-POMDP modeling with heuristic task scheduling, discrete grid exploration statistics, and Gazebo and ROS virtual multi-robot navigation integration. Benchmark cost is in discrete path steps and cannot be converted directly into seconds.
## Sensors
Discrete benchmark: a 5-cell field of view and geometric communication. Gazebo: each robot has a 360-degree LiDAR for gmapping localization and mapping and four RGB cameras for short-range dynamic obstacle avoidance. The method needs local SLAM, relative poses, and mergeable maps; it does not solve cross-robot map registration.

## Key quantitative results
- London, completion in steps: Ours 7760 +/- 345, OursSync 6996 +/- 116, BS 11818 +/- 1354, CRN2R 10558 +/- 827; OursSync is about 40.8% below BS and 33.7% below CRN2R in this simulated benchmark (p. 6, Table I).
- New York: OursSync 4436 +/- 117 versus BS 6992 +/- 648; Dubai: 7700 +/- 413 versus BS 14643 +/- 1273 (p. 6, Table I).
- Across 20 scenes the method mostly beats the relay baselines, but synchronized replanning is not better in every scene: on air01 and cons03 OursSync is slightly worse than Ours (p. 6, Table I).
- cons01 and London: mean DEC-POMDP proxy return about 2000 versus below 1000 for CRN2R; this is a utility metric, not coverage or accident rate.
- Gazebo: both runs completed exploration and rendezvous; no confidence intervals, large teams, or genetic-solver performance (p. 8).
## Stated limitations
- To update the rendezvous plan, the robots must have common information (p. 6).
- The Gazebo integration still needs a communication protocol to synchronize rendezvous events (p. 8).
## Transfer conditions
Legacy reviewer observations:
- The bounding-rectangle area of the mission zone is used as a prior for planning resources; an unknown map does not mean no boundary or area information.
- The optimized metrics correlate with the DEC-POMDP reward, but there is no proof of a globally optimal policy or of periodic full connectivity under navigation failures, timeouts, or permanent disconnection.
- Links are geometric radii without occlusion, multipath, throughput, or packet loss. Robots meeting a neighbor can exchange maps opportunistically; a rendezvous requires the sub-team to gather and communicate, after which a designated robot sends new positions, and a timeout skips to the next agreement.
- Map exchange, relative localization accuracy, and reachability of rendezvous locations are preconditions of execution.
- The baselines are author-designed and are not compared against modern complete collaborative exploration stacks.
- According to the legacy card, the JSSP does not yet model navigation and exploration uncertainty, and heterogeneous robots, energy constraints, and trajectory factors are future work (no page recorded).

## Original abstract
Communication constraints can significantly impact robots’ ability to share information, coordinate their movements, and synchronize their actions, thus limiting coordination in Multi-Robot Exploration (MRE) applications. In this work, we address these challenges by modeling the MRE application as a DEC-POMDP and designing a joint policy that follows a rendezvous plan. This policy allows robots to explore unknown environments while intermittently sharing maps opportunistically or at rendezvous locations without being constrained by joint path optimizations. To generate the rendezvous plan, robots represent the MRE task as an instance of the Job Shop Scheduling Problem (JSSP) and minimize JSSP metrics. They aim to reduce waiting times and increase connectivity, which correlates to the DEC-POMDP rewards and time to complete the task. Our simulation results suggest that our method is more efficient than using relays or maintaining intermittent communication with a base station, being a suitable approach for Multi-Robot Exploration. We developed a proof-ofconcept using the Robot Operating System (ROS) that is available at: https://github.com/multirobotplayground/ Noetic-Multi-Robot-Sandbox.
