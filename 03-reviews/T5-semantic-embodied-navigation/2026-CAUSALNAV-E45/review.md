## Problem

In dynamic outdoor scenes, an open-vocabulary natural-language goal must be grounded in a retrievable multi-level semantic map and connected to long-range routing and dynamic obstacle avoidance.

## Mechanism

YOLO-World and ByteTrack track objects in RGB, project them with calibrated LiDAR, and fuse them with FAST-LIO2 poses to build the Embodied Graph of objects, buildings, and hierarchical clusters. CenterPoint (TensorRT) and LIOsegmot trajectories form spatio-temporal corridors that remove dynamic entities. An LLM generates cluster semantics, and hierarchical retrieval-augmented generation retrieves candidates and reranks them by the current pose. Global routing uses Dijkstra on a graph of past trajectories, or offline roads or the Google Maps or Amap API when no history exists. RH-Map removes dynamic ghosting, and Informed-RRT* with B-splines and an NMPC-CBF controller tracks paths around predicted obstacles. The legacy card notes that the name CausalNav does not refer to statistical causal identification.

## Baselines and controls

NoMaD, ViNT, GNM, and CityWalker. The authors collected image data to build topological maps for NoMaD, ViNT, and GNM, and gave CityWalker the waypoints generated by CausalNav, so the systems receive different inputs (p. 6). LLM variants compared: phi4-14B, Qwen3-14B, Gemma3-12B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-14B, and GPT-4o. Ablations cover static versus dynamic graph updating and retrieval weights.

## Platform and setup

- Simulation: Gazebo urban ground robot with dynamic pedestrians and vehicles; 25 random tasks with 10 trials each (p. 6). Success means reaching within 10 m of the target (p. 6).
- Real world: campus deployment with two example runs, a 130 m object-level instruction and a 512 m building-level instruction (p. 7); the number of repetitions and a real success-rate distribution are not given.
- The real robot is labeled Agilex HUNTER SE in Fig. 5 (p. 6), a four-wheeled vehicle.
- The initial semantic graph is built by full-environment exploration and buildings come from an offline map, so this is not exploration of an unknown world without priors (p. 6).

## Evidence type and transfer

Quantitative comparisons are from simulation; hardware evidence is two closed-loop campus cases on a wheeled robot. Multi-day operation, a large-sample real success rate, and transfer to other chassis are not shown.

## Sensors

Simulation: RealSense D435i and a 3D LiDAR. Real robot: D435i RGB camera, RoboSense Helios LiDAR, and RTK GNSS/INS at 10 Hz and 5 cm; FAST-LIO2 is combined with RTK for coordinate transformation and localization. The method depends on LiDAR-camera calibration and on an offline map or prior exploration.

## Compute and latency

Simulation uses an i9-14900K and an RTX 3090; perception and ego-motion run at 30 Hz, trajectory corridors at 20 Hz, local map and planning at 10 Hz, and graph update and hierarchical clustering at 1 Hz. The real robot carries an i9-13900H CPU and an RTX 4070 GPU (p. 6). Table IV gives the mean per-cycle latency on the same hardware: CausalNav 105 ms, NoMaD 95 ms, ViNT 150 ms, GNM 110 ms, CityWalker 180 ms. The legacy card notes that 10 Hz is approximate, because 105 ms exceeds a strict 100 ms deadline; tail latency, real-robot GPU memory, and separate LLM latency are not reported.

## Key quantitative results

- Simulation, small / medium / large scenes (Table I): CausalNav SR 100 / 92 / 80%, SPL 88.9 / 82.2 / 66.0%, collisions 0.2 / 0.6 / 1.2, trajectory length 40.66 / 83.16 / 141.82 m; CityWalker SR 100 / 85 / 80%, collisions 1.2 / 3.4 / 4.5 (Table I, image; no located page).
- Graph updating (Table III): without versus with updating, SR 78 versus 90%, SPL 54.7 versus 80.1%, collisions 1.8 versus 1.1, trajectory length 120.35 versus 98.25 m (Table III, image; no located page).
- LLM choice (Table II): DeepSeek-R1-Distill-14B SR 85% versus GPT-4o 88%; the local model is slightly below the API model (Table II, image; no located page).
- Runtime (Table IV): mean per-cycle latency 105 ms for CausalNav versus 95 (NoMaD), 150 (ViNT), 110 (GNM), and 180 ms (CityWalker) (Table IV, image; no located page).
- Real world: in the 130 m case ViNT and CausalNav succeed; in the 512 m case only CausalNav completes the task (p. 7). No real success-rate statistics are given.

## Stated limitations

- CausalNav still faces limitations in scalability, robustness under extreme lighting or weather, and long-horizon consistency (p. 8).
- The authors list long-term autonomous exploration and lifelong learning as future work (p. 8 per the legacy card).

## Transfer conditions

Legacy reviewer observations:
- The 10 m success radius is loose; results cannot be compared directly with tasks that require close approach or object contact.
- Collisions remain in simulation, so the CBF formulation does not imply zero collisions under perception error.
- Initial full-environment exploration and offline building and road priors are required; whether the public-API routing fallback needs network access must be checked for each deployment.
- Two real cases without durations, repetitions, or confidence intervals do not support multi-day long-term autonomy.
- The real chassis is not the reference platform.

## Survey-usable statements

### Supported

- In Gazebo urban scenes with dynamic agents, a retrievable multi-level scene graph with dynamic-object filtering reaches equal or higher SR than CityWalker with fewer collisions (success radius 10 m).
- Online graph updating raises simulated SR from 78% to 90% compared with a static graph.

### Not supported

- CausalNav achieves long-term or multi-day autonomy: the hardware evidence is two runs, and the authors list long-term exploration as future work.
- CausalNav is collision-free: simulated collisions are 0.2-1.2 per run.
- The local LLM matches the API model: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-14B is at 85% versus 88% for GPT-4o.
- The system runs at a strict 10 Hz: the mean cycle is 105 ms.
- The baselines received identical inputs: they used image topological maps or CausalNav's waypoints.

## Original abstract

Autonomous language-guided navigation in largescale outdoor environments remains a key challenge in mobile robotics, due to difficulties in semantic reasoning, dynamic conditions, and long-term stability. We propose CausalNav, the first scene graph-based semantic navigation framework tailored for dynamic outdoor environments. We construct a multi-level semantic scene graph using LLMs, referred to as the Embodied Graph, that hierarchically integrates coarse-grained map data with finegrained object entities. The constructed graph serves as a retrievable knowledge base for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), enabling semantic navigation and long-range planning under openvocabulary queries. By fusing real-time perception with offline map data, the Embodied Graph supports robust navigation across varying spatial granularities in dynamic outdoor environments. Dynamic objects are explicitly handled in both the scene graph construction and hierarchical planning modules. The Embodied Graph is continuously updated within a temporal window to reflect environmental changes and support real-time semantic navigation. Extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world settings demonstrate superior robustness and efficiency.
