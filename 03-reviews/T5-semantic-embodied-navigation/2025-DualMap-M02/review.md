## Problem
Earlier semantic maps are closed-set, assume a static world, or are too slow for lifelong use: closed-set RGB-D maps cannot answer open queries, recent open-vocabulary systems often assume a static scene, and dynamic open-vocabulary pipelines can take hours to build a usable map. DualMap targets online open-vocabulary mapping and language-guided navigation when queried objects move, either within an anchor (in-anchor) or between anchors (cross-anchor relocation).
## Mechanism
A hybrid frontend combines YOLOv8-World, with a class list generated by GPT-4, and FastSAM, fused with MobileSAM masks. An intra-object stability check and object splitting let the system skip costly 3D inter-object merging. Observations update a fine-grained 3D concrete map, which is abstracted into an abstract map of typically static anchors (for example beds and desks) and the layout. Navigation uses the global abstract map for candidate selection and a local concrete map for goal reaching. When the queried object is missing, the abstract map is updated online and a new candidate is selected, up to an attempt limit. DualMap does not estimate pose: LiDAR Fast-LIO2 supplies external localization. Embeddings use MobileCLIP-S2.

## Platform and setup
- Mapping and efficiency: Replica and ScanNet at their original resolutions.
- Simulated language-guided object navigation: Habitat on HM3D scenes 00829, 00848, and 00880 with YCB objects. Success means stopping within 1 m of the object; in dynamic scenes the target must also be found within three attempts.
- Real robots: two unnamed platforms (a wheeled robot and a quadruped) share a rigid LiDAR and RGB-D mount (Fig. 6a) across four scenes (Table VII). The map is built while the robot is driven manually; the same navigation pipeline as in simulation is then run, and objects are rearranged by hand for the dynamic trials.
## Evidence type and transfer
Segmentation and efficiency come from public-dataset playback, the navigation success rates by relocation type from Habitat, and the per-scene static and dynamic success rates from closed-loop runs on the two real platforms. The real runs use manually driven mapping, external Fast-LIO2 pose, and hand-staged object relocation, so they show navigation after scene change, not mapping under autonomous exploration.

## Key quantitative results
- Replica mIoU: DualMap 0.2538 versus HOV-SG 0.2050 and ConceptGraphs 0.1501 (Table I).
- Replica peak memory: DualMap 4564 MB versus HOV-SG about 158127 MB (Table I).
- Replica time per frame: DualMap 0.276 s versus HOV-SG 42.0 s (Table I).
- Habitat average success: 70.5% static, 64.8% in-anchor, and 60.3% cross-anchor relocation.
- Cross-anchor failures: 28.3% false matches, 9.4% attempt limit, and 1.9% planning (p. 7).
- Real wheeled robot, static versus dynamic success: meeting room 85.7% versus 70.3%; apartment 69.6% versus 51.5% (Table VII).
- Real quadruped, static versus dynamic success: hallway 78.9% versus 55.6%; outdoor 75.0% versus 50.0% (Table VII).
## Stated limitations
- DualMap does not perform camera pose estimation and relies on external localization (p. 8).
- The system does not yet incorporate human-object interactions (p. 8).
- Even in simple outdoor scenes, performance degrades compared with indoor settings because of higher sensor noise (p. 8).
## Transfer conditions
Legacy reviewer observations: mapping still depends on RGB-D quality, and noisy real RGB-D is the reason for the stability check (not located). The reported compute is a desktop-class RTX 4090, not an onboard edge stack. A wheeled robot without a LiDAR odometry source must supply its own pose. The dynamic scenes are object relocations, not crowd navigation.

## Original abstract
We introduce DualMap, an online open-vocabulary mapping system that enables robots to understand and navigate dynamically changing environments through natural language queries. Designed for efficient semantic mapping and adaptability to changing environments, DualMap meets the essential requirements for real-world robot navigation applications. Our proposed hybrid segmentation frontend and object-level status check eliminate the costly 3D object merging required by prior methods, enabling efficient online scene mapping. The dual-map representation combines a global abstract map for high-level candidate selection with a local concrete map for precise goal-reaching, effectively managing and updating dynamic changes in the environment.Through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world scenarios, we demonstrate state-of-the-art performance in 3D open-vocabulary segmentation, efficient scene mapping, and online language-guided navigation.
