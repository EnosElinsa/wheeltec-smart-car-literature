# Evidence coding of the survey selection

This folder holds the coding layer that the survey uses for its degradation-evidence statistics. It codes the 245 works of the survey selection (244 technical works and one context review) for degradation source, the three degradation-evidence classes, task outcomes in the same experiment, evidence level, response family, platform group and drivetrain. The review cards in `03-reviews/` and the comparison tables of `06-data/` stay unchanged. For manuscript statistics, the codes of this folder replace the card fields `degradation`, `degradation_observed` and `evaluation_all`, and the card-level counts of the previous revision remain one sensitivity row.

## Provenance

The codebook was drafted in a language-model agent session on 2026-11-04, before any work was coded. It fixes the seed (20261105) and the stratified draw rule of the sample, and the draw is reproducible with `draw_sample` in `05-survey/manuscript/scripts/coding.py`.

Both coding passes were made by language-model sessions, and so was the adjudication. No human rater coded or adjudicated. The primary coding of all 245 works was made in a Kiro workflow session configured for the model claude-opus-5.5, from the review cards, the transcriptions and the PDFs. Every coded value that asserts presence carries a locator, while the given and derived columns (platform group, drivetrain, strongest evidence level, integrity and placement among them) follow the paper index and carry none. Values of light cards from the legacy batches are located on a physical PDF page, checked by a phrase or label search of that page. Every value of the other light cards has either a `pdf` locator on a physical page checked against the PDF text, or a `card` locator that names a block of the card and only pages that this block cites. `python scripts/coding.py --check-codes` enforces both rules (codebook Section 15). In the first coding pass, 117 light-card values carried card locators whose block cites no page or other pages. They were relocated on the PDF before the freeze, and the same page checks corrected three works (K13 `nominal_control`, M33 `drivetrain_effect`, and the R33 task-outcome attributes).

The second coding is a language-model second coding, not human inter-rater reliability. A separate Kiro workflow session configured for the model gpt-5.6-sol coded the double-coded attributes of a stratified random sample of 40 technical works, 8 per principal section, drawn by section and assessment depth with every selection stage represented. It received only the codebook (version 1.0), the sample list and the sampled works' review cards, transcriptions and PDFs, and it had no access to the primary codes. It finished at 13:41 on 2026-11-04, before the primary codes were committed at 14:48. Both coders read the same review cards, which were themselves prepared with language-model tools. The agreement therefore measures how consistently two language-model coders apply this codebook to shared evidence.

The models named above are the models configured for each workflow step, not confirmed executed models. The second coder's notes state that its session exposed no model identifier, and the primary-coding session recorded none either. A later review session of the same workflow, configured for gpt-5.6-terra, reported Claude Sonnet 5.5 in its own session context. The two coders may therefore have run on the same model or on related models. Codebook Section 19 states the design intent of a second coder on a different model. That intent is configured but not verified, and the codebook text stays unchanged because its hash is frozen.

## Agreement and adjudication

`coding-agreement.csv` reports, before adjudication, unweighted Cohen's kappa and percent agreement for every double-coded attribute, with the number of works and units and the categories used. Set-valued attributes are scored as one binary flag per value. The file gives the kappa of each flag (for functions over the 32 sampled works whose principal function differs from the flag), a kappa pooled over all flag decisions of the attribute, and the share of works with identical sets. Response family is compared on the primary family, as fixed in codebook version 1.0. Named sources (D1 or D2) and the publication class are derived from the three class columns.

The two codings differed on 112 work-attribute values in 35 of the 40 works. Each was resolved by re-reading the source at both coders' locators: the primary value was kept 58 times, the second value adopted 46 times and both revised 8 times, so 54 resolutions changed a primary code. 28 of the differences concerned `response_family`, 22 of them only the optional secondary family. Two rules caused disagreement in three or more sampled works and were clarified in codebook version 1.1. Response family now holds the primary family only. Eight primary codes carried a secondary family: two lost it through sampled disagreements and six in the recheck of all works. The drivetrain-effect clarification can only turn `yes` into `no`; the recheck read the six primary codes with `yes`, and all six keep it. A later review recheck searched the 239 works coded `no` for missed cases, through a keyword screen of every review card and transcription and a reading of each candidate passage on its PDF page. It added `yes` for four works (M04, M09, N10 and S16), which gives 10 works with a drivetrain-dependent effect. The resolved codes enter `generated/evidence-codes.csv` and every derived table through the adjudication log; `primary-codes.csv` stays as frozen.

## Review recheck

After an internal re-review of the survey draft, a Kiro workflow session rechecked two groups of final codes on the PDF pages and logged every decision with the scope `review-recheck`. These rows use three resolutions: `recheck-revised` for a changed value, `recheck-kept` for a value confirmed with a page locator, and `locator-corrected` for an unchanged value whose locator is replaced, with the replaced locator named in the reason. Every locator of these rows names a physical PDF page and was checked on that page.

The first group holds the 19 works whose `level_degradation` was `P` without a physical same-experiment conjunction. The level stays `P` when a physical closed-loop run under a named source reports a measured quantity of that run in the text, a table or a plotted measure. Video, photographs and trajectory overlays without such a quantity do not count (codebook Section 12). The level was kept for 17 works and lowered to `S` for F09 and N05, whose physical runs report no measured quantity. For five of the kept works (M12, N17, R29, R36 and S16), the text or a table of the physical run reports a task outcome under the named source. The recheck therefore recoded their `deg_task_setting` as `physical`, with `task_setting` `both` for M12, and with `task_setting` `physical` and `deg_task_class` `D2` for R29, whose personal-space violation time is a safety-constraint violation (codebook Section 10). E32 keeps `simulation` with a `recheck-kept` row, since its hardware runs under the emulated robot failure appear only as curves without stated numbers. The second group is the drivetrain recheck described above. The same session corrected the locators of two works. The D1 conjunction of E44 is reported in Fig. 5, not in Table II. Four values of R26 rest on the Hound result in Table II, not Table III. The R26 review card carries the same label correction.

`python scripts/coding.py --check-final` (run in `05-survey/manuscript`) validates the final codes with the final locators. Its rule CR21 requires `deg_task_setting` `physical` or a `recheck-kept` locator for every final `level_degradation` `P`, which makes the Section 12 rule checkable. It applies to the 12 kept works whose setting stays `simulation` or `none`. CR21 is a check of `scripts/coding.py` on the final codes that the frozen codebook text does not list. `build_data.py` and `verify_delivery.py` validate the final codes with the same final locators.

All files use UTF-8 without a byte-order mark and LF line endings. They contain no publisher text and no local paths.
