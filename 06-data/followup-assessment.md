## G1 — Localization or integrity-triggered recovery

**What the reading changes.** [F01](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2025-Line-Integrity-F01/review.md) supplies a RAIM-inspired line-map localization protection level and outlier rejection on recorded ground-robot and other data. Its authors say the protection level predicts state-estimation errors; it does not drive an alert threshold, stop, relocalization, or planner action (PDF p. 13). The campus reference and prior map both use a LiDAR/IMU estimation pipeline, so empirical error coverage there should not be described as independently measured integrity risk. [F04](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2024-Switch-SLAM-F04/review.md) does connect diagnosed LiDAR/visual weakness to a change of estimator initialization and optimization, including an IMU-supported case when both degrade. Its evidence is dataset playback and simulation with trajectory/detector outcomes, not a measured navigation recovery with collision or completion outcomes (PDF pp. 4–8). [F02](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-Event-FTC-F02/review.md) closes a different loop: a descriptor observer estimates modeled sensor/actuator faults for event-triggered setpoint tracking in a low-speed, no-slip AGV model and LAN-connected hardware-in-loop test (PDF pp. 2–3, 8–10). It does not show degraded localization triggering an autonomous navigation fallback or a terrain/slip recovery.

[F03](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Fault-Map-Update-F03/review.md) adds a concrete diagnosis-to-repair chain: residual tests isolate observation or prior-map faults, faulty observations are excluded, and inflation of a faulty landmark's covariance allows subsequent observations to correct its pose (PDF pp. 7–8). For odometry divergence, the specified response is a signal to reinitialize the filter or warn that localization is unusable; a reset execution trial is not established. Simulation and recorded data from three road vehicles evaluate localization/map quality and covariance consistency, including injected faults (PDF pp. 9–14). They do not demonstrate a recovery maneuver or a live estimate-to-control connection. Fault isolation requires redundant common-landmark observations and assumes at most two simultaneous faults; ambiguous or excessive faults can force exclusion without map repair (PDF p. 8). Real LiDAR noise also requires empirical false-alarm adjustment (PDF p. 13).

**Bounded conclusion.** The draft survey should acknowledge integrity monitoring, estimator switching, diagnosis-to-map repair, and fault-tolerant tracking as existing mechanisms. This selected follow-up set leaves their connection to navigation recovery and a paired safety-and-task evaluation unresolved. An incisive next test would inject measured localization degradation, record calibration/false alarms and detection-to-action time, and compare a defined stop, slow, relocalize, or replan policy against no-action and simple-threshold controls. Report both collision/intervention risk and completion/path/time under the same faults. Distinguish executed map/observation repair from the specified reset/warning response and from an evaluated navigation recovery.

## G2 — Model reasoning, collision constraints, query cost, fallback

**What the reading changes.** [F05](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2025-VL-TGS-F05/review.md) gives a real wheeled-robot interface: LiDAR/velocity candidates are overlaid on RGB for hosted GPT-4V selection, and DWA continues along the selected trajectory during model delay (PDF pp. 3–4, 7). The learned generator does not guarantee traversable candidates; the paper does not evaluate a model-timeout, network-loss, or bad-selection fallback. Its outdoor hardware comparison has one run per method, so it does not yield a robust failure probability. [F06](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2025-GSON-F06/review.md) routes group semantics from an LMM through a mid-level planner while local NMPC-CBF uses tracked pedestrians (PDF pp. 3–7). This is more than a pure prompt-to-motion pipeline, but occlusion, group-label errors, and second-scale inference constrain responsiveness. The selected tests do not establish a deadline-triggered fallback or an unconditional collision guarantee.

[F07](../03-reviews/T6-reliability-deployment/2025-Sentinel-F07/review.md) provides a complementary timing decomposition: statistical action-consistency monitoring targets erratic policy behavior, while slower cloud VLM analysis targets progress failures in mobile manipulation (PDF pp. 6, 8, 23, 35). Detection is measured, but an alarm-triggered navigation recovery is not; its formal nominal false-positive statement applies to STAC rather than the combined detector. [F08](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2025-Perceive-Confidence-F08/review.md) links learned occupancy uncertainty to an explicit safe planner and tests static-chair avoidance with an onboard quadruped (PDF pp. 4–8, 18). Its environment-level calibration and known-state/static-scene assumptions matter. It neither uses language reasoning nor resolves model-query budget or timeout behavior.

**Bounded conclusion.** Replace any blanket “model navigation lacks safety integration” wording with the narrower finding that these papers distribute semantics, fast local constraints, statistical perception assurance, and failure monitoring across different systems. The combined design question remains: define a query cadence and maximum age, measure response-time distribution and monetary/network cost, validate the state/obstacle assumptions of the local collision filter, and test explicit timeout, missed-detection, and unsafe-selection fallbacks. Pair safety outcomes with task completion and interventions, not just semantic-choice or detector accuracy. Do not treat DWA's continued-path behavior or an NMPC-CBF on imperfect tracks as a tested fail-safe policy.

## G3 — Pose uncertainty changes terrain decisions

**What the reading changes.** [F10](../03-reviews/T2-slam-mapping-exploration/2026-Multi-Fidelity-GP-F10/review.md) is a real counterexample to saying that localization uncertainty never enters exploration: it splits field samples by localization uncertainty and changes a GP reconstruction/trajectory objective in closed-loop miniature-fish tank experiments (PDF pp. 11–15). It is aquatic, uses externally supplied position, and reconstructs a light field rather than evaluating wheeled terrain traversability or speed. [F05](../03-reviews/T5-semantic-embodied-navigation/2025-VL-TGS-F05/review.md) makes geometric and semantic traversability affect outdoor wheeled trajectory choice but does not propagate pose covariance into that choice (PDF pp. 3–7). [F08](../03-reviews/T3-planning-control/2025-Perceive-Confidence-F08/review.md) calibrates perception occupancy errors and combines them with a safe planner, but the theory assumes known state and the hardware is legged in static scenes (PDF pp. 4–8). [F14](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Cross-Rank-F14/review.md) additionally shows that localization drift can erode the benefit of position-only team coordination; that is sensitivity to pose quality, not a pose-conditioned terrain-cost policy (PDF p. 19).

[F09](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Terrain-Voronoi-F09/review.md) supplies direct wheeled-terrain control evidence: local plane fitting, slope/height/continuity analysis, 3D Voronoi allocation, and greedy subgoal selection guide two physical Ackermann vehicles through four outdoor terrain scenes. The vehicles use Orin NX 8GB, RoboSense C16, A-LOAM, and a 0.6 m/s speed cap (PDF pp. 6–11, 13–16). Hardware completion is qualitative trajectory evidence; repeated baseline/ablation statistics belong to simulation using a different Scout drive model (PDF pp. 11–12). Crucially, the covariance in the terrain-plane calculation is the scatter of local point coordinates used for PCA plane fitting, not robot-pose covariance propagated into traversability or speed (PDF pp. 7–8). Thus terrain-aware wheeled collaboration is supported, while the specific uncertainty-to-terrain coupling remains open in this reading.

[F11](../03-reviews/T1-perception-localization/2026-M2UD-F11/review.md) provides M2UD, a localization/mapping benchmark with uneven terrain and degraded sensing, rather than an evaluated navigation controller. Main recordings use six-wheeled and four-wheeled ground robots; detailed mapping also includes a detachable handheld sensor subset (PDF pp. 7, 12). Outdoor trajectory references smooth RTK, indoor references use offline multi-sensor SLAM, and mixed routes add RTK factors. Only two representative indoor sequences receive an independent laser-tracker check (PDF pp. 11–12); FARO map references do not independently validate every localization trajectory. The EA-Drift metric combines pose error and frame computation time, without establishing end-to-end control delay or a risk/speed policy (PDF pp. 13–14). Future terrain/navigable-area semantic labels remain proposed (PDF p. 19). Sensor synchronization and reference dependence must be retained when choosing sequences for a new experiment.

**Bounded conclusion.** Geometry-based wheeled terrain navigation, pose-uncertainty-aware exploration on a boundary platform, and relevant benchmark data are established separately. Their combination as a pose-uncertainty-conditioned wheeled terrain decision is not established by these targeted cards. An appropriate experiment would propagate estimated pose uncertainty into traversability cost, footprint inflation, speed/braking limits, or action rejection; compare uncertainty-aware and fixed-cost planners on matched rough/uneven routes; and log pose error, hazard contact, intervention, speed, and task completion. Distinguish uncertainty in the *robot pose* from geometric point-set scatter, scene semantics, or occupancy; assess the independence and coverage of any reference trajectory.

## G4 — Communication loss, reunion, and local fault spread

**What the reading changes.** [F12](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Failure-Tracking-F12/review.md) responds differently to temporary and permanent sensing/communication failures. Connected drones plan jointly; isolated agents plan individually; a temporarily disconnected target estimate can be merged by covariance intersection on reunion (PDF pp. 5–8). A permanently communication-isolated drone instead forfeits tracking and circles to signal the hazard (PDF p. 10). Staged Crazyflie hardware trajectories have ground vehicles as *targets*, not wheeled robot controllers; the model assumes a uniformly connected league and effectively infinite range among its connected members (PDF pp. 5, 13–14). CI on target estimates is not a proof of shared-map consistency after an arbitrary partition.

[F13](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-C-CBF-F13/review.md) encodes estimated reception probability and collision distance in local QPs, including modeled jamming with ns-3/Gazebo/ArduPilot network-in-the-loop simulation (PDF pp. 4–8, 12–15). Its guarantee depends on channel/velocity-control premises; penalized slack can relax a barrier, and below-threshold links can be excluded from a local QP. The paper explicitly says this simulation does not replace a distributed physical deployment. [F14](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Cross-Rank-F14/review.md) demonstrates a lean position-history exchange in one outdoor three-Spot exploration mission with intermittent packets, but assumes a common frame; simulated localization drift harms coordination and the physical mission still receives messages (PDF pp. 5, 17–20). It does not demonstrate a sustained complete partition. [F15](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2025-CoVOR-SLAM-F15/review.md) trades inter-agent visual-feature/map exchange for poses, covariances, and ranges. Its strongest direct range/vision evidence is recorded data from a moving rover and a fixed UWB rover; a four-agent KITTI experiment splits one car sequence into virtual simultaneous agents with simulated ranges (PDF pp. 1–3, 6–11). Neither is closed-loop multi-rover outage recovery.

[F03](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Fault-Map-Update-F03/review.md) directly addresses differing map estimates, inter-robot correlations, and faulty shared observations. SKF retains cross-covariances with non-collaborating robots and KLA fuses full-state estimates before collaborative updates (PDF pp. 5–6). Fault-isolation and landmark repair can prevent estimator corruption under the stated redundancy/fault assumptions; a later collaborator can correct its map from repaired peer estimates (PDF pp. 8, 11, 13–14). The architecture assumes a common prior map/frame and landmark IDs, common-date observations, bidirectional communication, and buffering (PDF p. 3); KLA needs consistent incoming estimates. Standalone operation and message-loss tolerance are claimed, but changing collaboration windows do not constitute a controlled sustained-partition, stale-state, or reconnection experiment. This is meaningful fault-aware map fusion with bounded assumptions, without a joint outage/reunion/navigation-task evaluation.

[F09](../03-reviews/T4-multi-robot-coordination/2026-Terrain-Voronoi-F09/review.md) merges local point clouds and shares robot positions/maps through a Master-based ROS architecture on a common Wi-Fi LAN (PDF pp. 6, 15). The communication-radius restriction in Voronoi allocation does not establish measured packet-loss tolerance. The selected two-robot navigation tests do not inject disconnection, delayed reunion, erroneous map registration, or faulty shared poses (PDF pp. 11–16).

**Bounded conclusion.** The selected literature supplies mechanisms for mode changes, local link-aware motion, sparse messages, cooperative pose fusion, correlation handling, and conditional map-fault repair. It does not yet justify the stronger survey sentence that a wheeled team has been shown to preserve task performance *and* consistent local/shared state through sustained loss, reunion, and corrupted information. A decisive comparison would impose measured temporary and sustained partitions, asymmetric packet loss, stale or wrong local pose/range/map inputs, and controlled reconnection on a moving ground-robot team. Track local versus shared state divergence, merge consistency, fault spread, task coverage/tracking, safety, message volume, and recovery delay. Include F03's repair/fusion mechanism among candidate controls while respecting its landmark and fault-count assumptions.

## Reading and citation rule

All fifteen linked cards are *light*, targeted reviews. They are useful for framing claims and selecting experiments, not a full-paper/table audit. Reopen the cited PDF pages and rendered figures/tables before importing a numeric effect size or a “best method” ranking into the manuscript. F03, F09, and F11 now contribute selected-page evidence, with source bytes and reading scope recorded. No comparison here establishes global absence, formal impossibility, or cross-paper performance ranking under matched conditions.
